Chapter 2: 10 Actionable Strategies from Dolores González’s AI Art Playbook

Dolores González doesn’t just create AI art—she teaches others how to do it effectively. Below are 10 proven strategies from her work, adapted for artists, designers, and businesses.

1. Start with a Strong "Artistic DNA" Before Training AI

Problem: Many artists jump into AI tools without defining their unique style, leading to generic, forgettable outputs.

González’s Approach:

  • Before using AI, curate a portfolio of your best work (even if it’s just sketches or digital experiments).
  • Train your AI model on these pieces to ensure it learns your textures, color palettes, and composition styles.
  • Example: If you specialize in biomechanical surrealism, feed the AI 100+ high-quality examples of your work before generating new pieces.

Real-World Example: A client of AI Mosaic Studio, Lena Chen, was struggling with AI-generated portraits that lacked her signature ethereal lighting. By training the AI on 20 of her best digital paintings, the system now produces portraits with her distinct glow effect—something traditional AI tools couldn’t replicate.

Action Step:

  • Step 1: Collect 10-20 of your strongest pieces (even if they’re not "perfect").
  • Step 2: Use tools like Stable Diffusion’s "LoRA" (Low-Rank Adaptation) to fine-tune an AI model on your style.
  • Step 3: Test prompts like:

    "A cyberpunk cityscape in the style of [Your Name], ultra-detailed, cinematic lighting, 8K"

2. Use "Constraint-Based Prompting" for Deeper Creativity

Problem: AI generates art based on vague prompts, leading to over-saturated, generic images.

González’s Solution: Instead of broad prompts like "a fantasy landscape," she uses "constraint-based prompting"—limiting variables to force unexpected, high-quality results.

Example Prompts She Uses:

  • "A medieval knight, but with biomechanical armor, neon accents, and a broken sword made of glass, ultra-detailed, 8K, cinematic lighting."
  • "A portrait of a woman, but her face is a mosaic of tiny portraits of famous artists, hyper-realistic, 16K."

Why It Works:

  • Reduces AI "hallucinations" (when AI invents details that don’t exist).
  • Encourages unique, memorable compositions.

Action Step:

  • Step 1: Pick a basic subject (e.g., "a cat").
  • Step 2: Add 3-5 constraints:
    • "A cat wearing a 1920s flapper dress, sitting on a floating bookshelf, oil painting style, golden hour lighting."
  • Step 3: Refine based on the AI’s output.

3. Leverage "Hybrid Workflows" – AI as a Collaborator, Not a Replacement

Problem: Some artists fear AI will replace their skills, leading to resistance rather than innovation.

González’s Hybrid Approach: She treats AI as a "digital assistant"—not a replacement for human creativity, but a tool to amplify it.

How She Does It:

  1. Sketch a rough concept (even a quick doodle).
  2. Use AI to expand it (e.g., turning a rough sketch into a high-res mosaic-style image).
  3. Manually refine the details (e.g., adjusting colors, adding textures).

Real-World Example: Ethan Park, a concept artist for a AAA game studio, used AI Mosaic Studio to generate 500+ background variations in hours—something that would take him weeks manually. He then selected the best 20 and refined them in Photoshop, blending AI efficiency with his hand-painted textures.

Action Step:

  • Step 1: Start with a rough digital sketch (even in Procreate or Krita).
  • Step 2: Use AI to expand it (e.g., "Turn this sketch into a mosaic-style cityscape, ultra-detailed, 4K").
  • Step 3: Manually tweak the final piece (colors, lighting, fine details).

4. Optimize for "Emotional Resonance" – The Human Touch in AI Art

Problem: AI-generated art often feels cold and mechanical because it lacks emotional depth.

González’s Secret: She trains AI on art that evokes emotions—whether it’s nostalgia, awe, or unease—and then guides the AI to replicate that feeling.

How She Does It:

  • Step 1: Study masterpieces that move you (e.g., Caravaggio’s chiaroscuro, Hieronymus Bosch’s surrealism).
  • Step 2: Train AI on these pieces to understand emotional composition.
  • Step 3: Use emotion-focused prompts:

    "A lonely astronaut on Mars, feeling isolation, moody lighting, textured surfaces, cinematic depth of field."

Real-World Example: Sophia Lee, a book cover artist, used AI Mosaic Studio to generate cover art for a dystopian novel. By training the AI on cyberpunk films like Blade Runner 2049 and painters like Zdzisław Beksiński, she created covers that felt "alive"—something her previous AI tools couldn’t achieve.

Action Step:

  • Step 1: Pick 3 emotional artworks that inspire you (e.g., a melancholic landscape, a surreal nightmare scene).
  • Step 2: Train AI on these pieces.
  • Step 3: Use prompts like:

    "A haunting forest at midnight, feeling of dread, moonlight filtering through trees, hyper-detailed, 8K."

5. Repurpose AI Art Across Multiple Platforms (Without Losing Quality)

Problem: AI-generated art often loses quality when resized for social media, print, or NFTs.

González’s Solution: She generates art in multiple resolutions and optimizes it for each platform using AI upscaling and texture mapping.

How She Does It:

Platform Resolution Optimization Technique
Social Media (Instagram, Twitter) 1080x1080px Lightweight JPG, 80% quality
Web Banners (Facebook Ads) 1200x628px PNG with transparency
Print (Posters, Books) 3000x3000px High-res TIFF, CMYK color profile
NFTs (OpenSea, Rarible) 1024x1024px Square format, 1MB max

Real-World Example: Marcus Chen, a digital marketer, used AI Mosaic Studio to generate ad banners for a crypto startup. By upscaling the original 4K image to 3000x3000px and converting it to CMYK, his print ads looked just as sharp as the digital version.

Action Step:

  • Step 1: Generate one high-res (4K) AI art piece.
  • Step 2: Use Photoshop’s "Smart Objects" to resize it for different platforms.
  • Step 3: Apply platform-specific optimizations:
    • Social Media: Compress with TinyPNG.
    • Print: Convert to CMYK in Adobe Color.
    • NFTs: Use NFT.solutions for batch optimization.

Problem: Many artists don’t know how to legally monetize AI-generated work.

González’s Model: She uses "dynamic licensing"—adjusting permissions based on usage (e.g., social media vs. commercial use).

How She Structures It:

License Type Price Usage Rights
Basic (Social Media) $20 Unlimited posts, no commercial use
Commercial (Ads, Merch) $150 Full commercial rights
Exclusive (NFT, High-End Art) $500+ One-time sale, no reprints

Real-World Example: Jamie Rodriguez, a freelance illustrator, started selling AI-generated art on Etsy using AI Mosaic Studio. By offering three license tiers, she increased her revenue by 300% compared to selling single-use prints.

Action Step:

  • Step 1: Decide on 3 license tiers (Basic, Commercial, Exclusive).
  • Step 2: Use Gumroad or Payhip to sell digital licenses.
  • Step 3: Offer bundles (e.g., "10 AI art pieces for $100").

7. Use AI to "Reverse-Engineer" Traditional Art Techniques

Problem: Some artists want to experiment with traditional techniques (e.g., watercolor, oil painting) but lack the skills.

González’s Hack: She trains AI on traditional art and then uses it to generate "digital approximations" of these styles.

How She Does It:

  1. Find high-res examples of the technique (e.g., watercolor paintings on ArtStation).
  2. Train AI on these examples.
  3. Generate new pieces in that style.

Real-World Example: Lena Kim, a digital painter, wanted to practice watercolor but struggled with real brushwork. By training AI on watercolor reference images, she generated digital watercolor-style art that looked hand-painted—something she couldn’t achieve manually in hours.

Action Step:

  • Step 1: Find 10 high-res examples of a traditional technique (e.g., woodcut prints, pointillism).
  • Step 2: Train AI on these images.
  • Step 3: Generate new art in that style and refine it manually.
