8 Actionable Strategies to Harness AI Like Dolores González

Dolores González’s work isn’t just about creating AI art—it’s about using AI as a force multiplier for human creativity. Below are eight proven strategies you can apply, whether you're an artist, developer, or business leader.

1. Start with a Strong Concept Before Using AI

Problem: Many artists jump straight into AI tools without a clear vision, leading to generic, uninspired outputs.

González’s Approach:

  • Before using AI, define your artistic intent. Ask:
    • What emotion or message do I want to convey?
    • What traditional techniques can I combine with AI?
    • What’s the unique "human touch" I’ll add?

Example: A painter using DALL·E 3 to create a fantasy landscape might first sketch a rough concept on paper, then refine it with AI-generated elements (e.g., unrealistic but harmonious colors).

Action Step:

  • Spend 20–30 minutes brainstorming before generating AI art.
  • Use AI as a collaborator, not a replacement for your vision.

2. Train Your AI on Diverse, High-Quality Data

Problem: AI models trained on limited or biased datasets produce predictable or offensive outputs.

González’s Solution:

  • Curate a diverse dataset for your AI. If you're working with portrait generation, include:
    • Different ethnicities, ages, and genders
    • Various lighting conditions and expressions
    • Historical and contemporary styles (e.g., Renaissance portraits vs. modern digital art)

Real-World Example: The AI Mosaic Studio team trained their Neural Brush model on thousands of masterpiece fragments from the Metropolitan Museum of Art, ensuring the AI could mimic different artistic movements while maintaining originality.

Action Step:

  • If using pre-trained AI models, fine-tune them with your own dataset.
  • For custom AI art tools, invest in high-resolution, diverse training data.

3. Experiment with Hybrid Workflows (AI + Traditional Media)

Problem: Purely AI-generated art can feel cold and mechanical, lacking the emotional depth of human-made pieces.

González’s Hybrid Approach:

  • Combine AI-generated elements with traditional techniques to create unique, textured artworks.
    • Example: Use AI to generate a color palette, then paint it by hand.
    • Example: AI creates a rough sketch, but you add intricate details with ink or watercolors.

Real-World Example: In her "AI & Acrylics" series, González used MidJourney to generate abstract shapes, then painted over them with thick acrylic layers, resulting in textured, tactile masterpieces.

Action Step:

  • Try "AI as a sketchpad"—use it for initial ideas, then refine with physical media.
  • Experiment with AI-generated textures (e.g., watercolor splashes, brushstrokes) and print them onto canvas.

4. Use AI for Iterative Refinement, Not Just Generation

Problem: Many artists treat AI as a "one-and-done" tool, generating an image and calling it final.

González’s Iterative Process:

  • AI should be part of a feedback loop:
    1. Generate a base concept (e.g., a fantasy creature).

    4. Regenerate with new parameters until the piece feels complete.

Common Mistakes in AI Art (And How to Avoid Them)

Even the most talented artists make pitfalls when using AI. Here are the biggest mistakes and how to fix them:

❌ Mistake 1: Over-Reliance on AI Without Human Touch

Problem: AI can generate perfect but soulless art if not curated by a human.

Solution:

  • Always add a "human layer"—whether it’s hand-drawn details, textured brushwork, or emotional storytelling.
  • Example: González never uses AI for entire paintings—she uses it for 30% of the work, then finishes manually.

❌ Mistake 2: Ignoring AI Limitations (e.g., Poor Text Handling)

Problem: Some AI tools struggle with text, fine details, or complex compositions.

Solution:

  • Break projects into steps:
    • Use AI for backgrounds or abstract elements.
    • Use traditional tools for text, faces, or intricate details.
  • Example: If generating a book cover, use AI for the background, then manually add the title.

❌ Mistake 3: Copying AI Outputs Without Refinement

Problem: Many artists download an AI image and call it "done"—leading to generic, unoriginal work.

Solution:

  • Treat AI as a "starting point"—then edit, recolor, and reshape it.
  • Example: González takes an AI-generated portrait, changes the color palette, and adds expressive brushstrokes.

❌ Mistake 4: Not Experimenting with Different AI Tools

Problem: Sticking to one AI generator limits creativity.

Solution:

  • Test multiple tools (e.g., DALL·E 3, MidJourney, Stable Diffusion, Runway ML).
