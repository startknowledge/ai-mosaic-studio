10 Actionable Strategies Inspired by Dolores González’s AI Art Mastery

Dolores González doesn’t just rely on cutting-edge AI—she teaches others how to use it effectively. Below are 10 proven strategies from her playbook that you can apply to your creative projects.

1. Start with a Clear Creative Vision Before Using AI

Problem: Many artists jump into AI tools without defining their end goal, leading to generic, uninspired outputs.

Dolores’ Approach: Before generating any AI-assisted art, she clearly defines the purpose, mood, and style of the project. For example:

  • If creating a brand logo, she specifies: "Modern, futuristic, with a touch of organic texture."
  • If designing social media graphics, she sets parameters like: "Minimalist, high-contrast, with a 2020s aesthetic."

How to Apply This:

  • Step 1: Write down 3 key adjectives that describe your desired outcome.
  • Step 2: Use AI prompts that align with these descriptors (e.g., "Generate a cyberpunk-inspired portrait of a futuristic scientist with neon blue accents").
  • Step 3: Refine the AI’s output by iterating based on feedback.

Real-World Example: A client wanted a unique album cover for an indie band. Instead of using a vague prompt like "rock band," Dolores guided them to specify:

  • "Vintage vinyl texture, 1970s psychedelic colors, with a hidden geometric pattern in the background." The result? A highly personalized, marketable design that stood out in a crowded genre.

2. Combine AI with Traditional Art Techniques for Hybrid Creativity

Problem: Pure AI-generated art can sometimes lack depth and emotional resonance compared to human-crafted pieces.

Dolores’ Approach: She believes in AI as a collaborator, not a replacement. Her workflow often involves:

  1. Generating a base AI concept (e.g., a rough sketch or color palette).
  2. Manually refining it with brushstrokes, textures, or hand-drawn elements.
  3. Using AI to enhance specific details (e.g., realistic shading, dynamic lighting).

How to Apply This:

  • Tool Suggestion: Use MidJourney for initial concepts, then Photoshop/Procreate for manual touches.
  • Example: If designing a fantasy character, generate a rough AI draft, then add custom armor details or expressive facial features by hand.

Real-World Example: A digital illustrator used AI to create a background scene for a game, but the characters felt "flat." Dolores advised:

  • "Let AI handle the environment, but draw the characters yourself—give them soul." The final result was a visually stunning but emotionally engaging world.

3. Leverage AI for Mood Boards & Concept Development

Problem: Brainstorming creative directions can be time-consuming and overwhelming.

Dolores’ Approach: She uses AI to quickly generate mood boards by inputting keywords like:

  • "Dark fantasy, gothic architecture, eerie lighting"
  • "Minimalist travel poster, 1960s aesthetic, warm tones"

This helps visualize themes before committing to a full design.

How to Apply This:

  • Step 1: Use DALL·E or Stable Diffusion to generate 5-10 variations of your concept.
  • Step 2: Analyze which styles resonate most.
  • Step 3: Refine your final prompt based on the best results.

Real-World Example: A fashion brand wanted to rebrand with a retro-futuristic theme. Instead of guessing, they used AI to test:

  • "1950s sci-fi fashion, metallic fabrics, holographic accents"
  • "Cyberpunk 2077-inspired streetwear, neon colors, futuristic silhouettes" The AI outputs helped them narrow down the perfect direction before hiring illustrators.

4. Use AI to Generate Custom Textures & Patterns

Problem: Creating unique textures (e.g., for fabric, walls, or backgrounds) can be tedious.

Dolores’ Approach: She uses AI texture generators to produce high-resolution, customizable patterns in seconds. For example:

  • "Abstract watercolor splatter texture for a fantasy map"
  • "Vintage paper grain overlay for a retro logo"

How to Apply This:

  • Tools: TextureLab, AI Texture Generator, or even MidJourney with specific texture prompts.
  • Pro Tip: Combine AI textures with manual edits in Photoshop for a hybrid look.

Real-World Example: A game developer needed unique wall textures for their open-world game. Instead of manually painting each surface, they used AI to generate:

  • "Medieval castle stonework with moss and cracks"
  • "Futuristic cityscape with glowing neon signs" This saved hundreds of hours while maintaining high visual fidelity.

5. Optimize AI Prompts for Better Results (The "Dolores Method")

Problem: Poorly worded AI prompts lead to misaligned or low-quality outputs.

Dolores’ Approach: She follows a structured prompt formula to maximize AI accuracy:

  1. Style: "Cyberpunk, ultra-detailed, cinematic lighting"
  2. Subject: "A lone astronaut in a ruined city"
  3. Details: "Hyper-realistic, metallic armor, glowing holographic interface"
  4. Mood: "Solemn, yet hopeful, with a touch of nostalgia"

How to Apply This:

  • Avoid vague terms like "cool colors"—instead, say "electric blue, teal, and deep purple gradient".
  • Use negative prompts to exclude unwanted elements (e.g., "no blurry edges, no cartoonish faces").
  • Test variations—AI responses can differ based on slight prompt changes.

Real-World Example: An artist tried to generate "a fantasy dragon" but got generic, low-detail results. Dolores adjusted the prompt to: "A majestic, winged dragon with intricate scales, glowing red eyes, standing on a crumbling stone pedestal, hyper-detailed, 8K resolution" The result? A stunning, highly detailed piece ready for commercial use.

6. Batch-Generate Variations for Faster Decision-Making

Problem: Choosing between multiple design options can slow down workflows.

Dolores’ Approach: She generates 10-20 variations at once using AI’s "variations" or "upscale" features, then quickly filters the best options.

How to Apply This:

  • Step 1: Generate 5-10 base images with slight variations in prompts.
  • Step 2: Use AI’s "best of" feature (if available) to refine the strongest candidates.
  • Step 3: Select the top 2-3 and manually enhance them.

Real-World Example: A marketing agency needed 10 different social media post designs for a product launch. Instead of manually creating each one, they used AI to generate:

  • "Product mockup with lifestyle background, bright and cheerful"
  • "Product mockup with dark, moody background, cinematic lighting" This allowed them to test multiple styles in minutes and pick the best-performing ones.

7. Use AI for Dynamic Backgrounds & Parallax Effects

Problem: Creating depth and movement in digital art requires advanced skills.

Dolores’ Approach: She uses AI-generated layers to simulate parallax effects (e.g., for web banners, game UIs, or animated graphics). For example:

  • "A futuristic cityscape with moving neon signs, shallow depth of field"
  • "A space scene with stars, planets, and a spaceship moving forward"

How to Apply This:

  • Tools: MidJourney (for static layers) + After Effects (for animation).
  • Pro Tip: Generate multiple depth layers (foreground, midground, background) separately, then combine them in Photoshop.

Real-World Example: A mobile game developer needed dynamic backgrounds for their app. Instead of hiring an animator, they used AI to generate:

  • "A scrolling forest with trees, animals, and a rising sun"
  • "A space battle with exploding ships and asteroid fields" These were then layered in Unity to create smooth, engaging animations.

8. Repurpose AI-Generated Art Across Multiple Platforms

Problem: Creating unique content for every platform (Instagram, TikTok, websites) is time-consuming.

Dolores’ Approach: She generates one AI piece and adapts it for multiple uses:

  • Instagram Post: Crop and adjust colors for square/portrait format.
  • TikTok Thumbnail: Add bold text overlays for engagement.
  • Website Banner: Extend the design with additional elements for depth.

How to Apply This:

  • Step 1: Create one high-quality AI image.
  • Step 2: Use Photoshop’s "Content-Aware Scale" to resize without distortion.
  • Step 3: Add platform-specific elements (e.g., hashtags for Instagram, CTAs for ads).

Real-World Example: A content creator used one AI-generated fantasy landscape to:

  • Instagram: Posted a square version with a caption.
  • Pinterest: Created a vertical pin with a call-to-action.
  • Website: Used it as a hero banner with text overlay. This maximized reach with minimal extra work.

9. Train AI Models on Your Brand’s Style (Customization)

Problem: Generic AI tools don’t always match brand aesthetics.

Dolores’ Approach: She trains AI models using existing brand assets (logos, color palettes, typography) to ensure consistent outputs. For example:

  • If a brand uses specific fonts and colors, she feeds those into the AI to generate cohesive designs.

How to Apply This:

  • Tools: Stable Diffusion’s "LoRA" (Low-Rank Adaptation) or custom datasets.
  • Step 1: Collect 10-20 high-quality brand images.
  • Step 2: Train the AI on these samples.
  • Step 3: Generate new designs that automatically align with your brand.

Real-World Example: A luxury fashion brand wanted AI-generated product images that matched their high-end aesthetic. Instead of using generic AI, they:

  • Trained the model on their past catalog images (specific lighting, textures, poses).
  • Generated new product shots that blended seamlessly with their existing visual identity.

10. Monetize AI-Generated Art with Smart Licensing

Problem: Many artists struggle to protect and profit from AI-generated work.

Dolores’ Approach: She advises using AI as a tool, not the final product, to avoid copyright issues while still monetizing. Strategies include:

  • Selling "AI-assisted" designs (e.g., "This piece was created with AI and hand-refined by the artist").
  • Offering custom AI generation services for clients.
  • Using watermarks and NDA agreements for commercial use.

How to Apply This:

  • Step 1: Clearly label AI-generated vs. handcrafted work.
  • Step 2: Use Creative Commons licenses or custom contracts for clients.
  • Step 3: Sell AI templates (e.g., "Buy this AI-generated texture pack and customize it!").

Real-World Example: An illustrator started an Etsy shop selling "AI-generated fantasy maps" but faced copyright concerns. Dolores advised:

  • "Sell the concept, not the AI output."
  • Instead, she offered custom map designs where the artist manually refined AI-generated drafts. This allowed her to charge premium prices while avoiding legal risks.

Solution:

  • Always add manual refinements (e.g., textures, emotions, unique details).
  • Use AI for inspiration, not execution.
