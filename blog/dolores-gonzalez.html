8 Actionable Strategies from Dolores González’s AI Art Approach

If you’re looking to incorporate AI into your creative workflow—or simply want to understand how to think like an AI artist—González’s methods offer practical, replicable strategies. Below are eight key techniques she uses (and how you can apply them).

1. Start with a Strong "Seed Concept" Before Training AI Models

What It Means: González emphasizes that AI art is only as good as the input it receives. Before training a model, she defines a "seed concept"—a clear, structured idea that guides the AI’s output.

How She Does It:

  • She begins with sketches, mood boards, or even written prompts that define the emotional tone, color palette, and stylistic elements she wants.
  • For example, if creating a cyberpunk-inspired landscape, she might provide:
    • A reference image of a neon-lit city.
    • A palette of electric blues and purples.
    • A description of "futuristic decay with organic elements."

How You Can Apply It:

  • For digital artists: Before using an AI tool like MidJourney or Stable Diffusion, write a detailed prompt (5-10 sentences) outlining your vision.
  • For designers: Use AI to generate variations of a logo or UI element, but constrain the output with specific rules (e.g., "minimalist, 1950s retro, limited to 3 colors").
  • For businesses: If using AI for marketing visuals, define a brand style guide (fonts, colors, tone) and feed it to the AI to ensure consistency.

Real-World Example: A client once asked González to create AI-generated illustrations for a children’s book. Instead of letting the AI freeform, she:

  1. Provided hand-drawn sketches of the characters.
  2. Defined the story’s color scheme (warm, earthy tones).
  3. Limited the AI to specific artistic movements (e.g., "watercolor-style illustrations with soft edges").

The result? A cohesive, emotionally resonant series of images that aligned perfectly with the book’s theme.

2. Use "Hybrid Workflows" – Combine AI with Traditional Techniques

What It Means: González believes the future of art lies in fusion—not in AI replacing human skill, but in AI augmenting it. She often starts with AI-generated drafts and then refines them with traditional tools.

How She Does It:

  • She uses AI to generate initial concepts (e.g., a rough sketch or color palette).
  • Then, she manually adjusts elements like:
    • Brushstrokes (adding texture in Photoshop).
    • Composition (rearranging elements for better balance).
    • Emotional depth (enhancing expressions or lighting).

How You Can Apply It:

  • For painters: Use AI to sketch a background, then paint over it with acrylics or oils.
  • For graphic designers: Let AI generate typography variations, then refine the kerning and hierarchy manually.
  • For 3D artists: Use AI to pose characters, then manually animate their expressions.

Real-World Example: In a 2022 collaboration with a fashion brand, González needed AI-generated fabric textures. She:

  1. Trained a custom model on high-resolution fabric scans.
  2. Used the AI to generate 50 texture variations.
  3. Then, selected the best 10 and hand-edited them in Photoshop to match the brand’s aesthetic.

The final textures were so realistic that clients mistook them for photographed samples.

3. Train Custom AI Models for Niche Applications

What It Means: Most AI art tools (like DALL·E) are general-purpose. González, however, trains custom models tailored to specific needs—whether for a brand, a personal style, or a unique artistic movement.

How She Does It:

  • She collects thousands of reference images (e.g., her own work, public domain art, or client-provided samples).
  • Uses fine-tuning techniques (like LoRA in Stable Diffusion) to teach the AI her distinctive style.
  • Tests the model with various prompts to ensure consistency.

How You Can Apply It:

  • For artists: If you have a signature style, train a model on your past work to generate new pieces in the same vein.
  • For businesses: Create a custom AI model for your brand’s visual identity (e.g., a hotel chain could train a model on its past marketing images).
  • For educators: Develop an AI tutor that generates practice problems in the style of a specific textbook.

Real-World Example: A luxury watch brand hired González to create AI-generated watch dials. Instead of using a generic AI, she:

How You Can Apply It:

How She Does It:

How You Can Apply It:

7. Ethical AI Art: Avoiding Copyright and Originality Issues

What It Means: AI art is a legal gray area, with debates over copyright, originality, and fair use. González is proactive about ethics, ensuring her work respects creators and avoids legal pitfalls.

How She Does It:

  • She only uses public domain or licensed reference images for training.
  • She credits human artists when her AI mimics their styles.
  • She avoids generating deepfakes or misinformation in her work.

How You Can Apply It:

  • For artists: If using AI, cite your sources and avoid directly copying copyrighted work.
  • For businesses: Use AI-generated assets but ensure they don’t infringe on trademarks.
  • For educators: Teach students about AI ethics to prevent unintentional plagiarism.

Real-World Example: A clothing brand wanted González to create AI-generated fashion illustrations. Instead of using real designer sketches, she:
