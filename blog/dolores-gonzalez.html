8 Actionable Strategies from Dolores González’s AI Artistry Framework

Dolores doesn’t just talk about AI—she teaches practical, implementable strategies that artists and businesses can use today. Below are eight proven techniques from her methodology:

1. The "AI-Assisted Brainstorming" Method

Problem: Many artists struggle with creative blocks because they rely too much on their own imagination.

Dolores’s Solution: Instead of starting with a blank canvas, use AI as a brainstorming partner. Tools like MidJourney, DALL·E, or Stable Diffusion can generate hundreds of concepts in seconds.

How to Apply It:

  • Step 1: Define a vague idea (e.g., "a futuristic city with neon lights and robots").
  • Step 2: Feed it into an AI prompt generator (e.g., "Generate 10 unique cyberpunk cityscapes").
  • Step 3: Let AI spark inspiration—then refine the best ideas manually.

Real-World Example: A graphic designer working on a brand identity for a tech startup used Dolores’s method to generate 50 logo concepts in under an hour. Instead of spending days sketching, they narrowed down the best options and refined them with AI-assisted tweaks.

2. The "Layered AI Collaboration" Technique

Problem: Some artists fear that AI will replace their work rather than enhance it.

Dolores’s Solution: AI should be seen as a "digital collaborator"—not a replacement. By layering AI-generated elements with human touch, you create unique, high-value art.

How to Apply It:

  • Step 1: Use AI to generate backgrounds, textures, or character designs.
  • Step 2: Manually adjust colors, proportions, and storytelling elements.
  • Step 3: Combine AI and human work in Photoshop, Procreate, or Blender.

Real-World Example: An illustrator working on a children’s book used AI to generate rough character sketches, then redrew them by hand with emotional depth. The result? A hybrid AI-human artwork that was more expressive than either method alone.

3. The "Prompt Engineering Blueprint"

Problem: Many users struggle to get high-quality AI outputs because their prompts are too vague or complex.

Dolores’s Solution: She developed a structured prompt framework that ensures clear, high-fidelity results.

Her Prompt Formula:

[Subject] + [Style] + [Mood] + [Details] + [Constraints]

Example Prompt: "A cyberpunk samurai in a neon-lit Tokyo, hyper-detailed, cinematic lighting, ultra-realistic, 8K, no faces, inspired by Blade Runner 2049, ultra-detailed armor, futuristic katana, 100mm lens, dramatic shadows."

How to Apply It:

  • Break prompts into logical parts.
  • Use specific adjectives ("hyper-detailed," "cinematic").
  • Avoid ambiguity—AI responds best to clear instructions.

Real-World Example: A game developer was frustrated with blurry, low-detail AI character models. By following Dolores’s prompt structure, they doubled the quality of their assets in half the time.

Problem: Artists want to replicate famous art styles (e.g., Van Gogh, Picasso) but lack the technical skills.

Dolores’s Solution: Use AI style transfer tools (like DeepDream, NightCafe, or Runway ML) to apply artistic filters to your work.

How to Apply It:

Real-World Example: A street artist wanted to create a limited-edition print in the style of Banksy. Using AI style transfer, they applied Banksy’s signature graffiti aesthetic to their digital piece, resulting in a high-demand NFT collection.

Common Mistakes in AI Artistry (And How to Avoid Them)

Even the most talented artists make critical errors when integrating AI into their workflows. Here are the biggest pitfalls—and how Dolores González helps artists avoid them.

❌ Mistake 1: Treating AI as a "Magic Button"

What Happens? Some artists expect AI to do all the work, leading to low-quality, generic outputs.

Dolores’s Fix:

  • AI is a tool, not a replacement—always refine and edit AI-generated work.
  • Use AI for inspiration, not final output (unless you’re working in a highly stylized, non-realistic medium).

Example: A concept artist uploaded a prompt for "a fantasy dragon" and used the AI output directly in a game. The result? A blurry, poorly proportioned dragon that failed client approval.

❌ Mistake 2: Ignoring Copyright & Ethical AI Use

What Happens? Some AI tools train on copyrighted data, leading to legal risks if used improperly.

Dolores’s Fix:

  • Use open-source AI models (e.g., Stable Diffusion WebUI, ComfyUI).
  • Check licensing before using AI-generated assets in commercial projects.
  • Credit AI tools when necessary (e.g., "Generated with MidJourney v6").

Example: A branding agency used an unlicensed AI model to create logo variations for a client. When the client’s competitor claimed copyright infringement, they faced legal consequences.

❌ Mistake 3: Over-Reliance on Default AI Settings

What Happens? Most AI tools have default settings that produce mediocre results.

Dolores’s Fix:

  • Experiment with different AI models (e.g., DALL·E vs. MidJourney vs. Stable Diffusion).
  • Adjust parameters (e.g., CFG scale, seed values, aspect ratios).
  • Use "negative prompts" to exclude unwanted elements (e.g., "no blurry edges, no low resolution").

Example: A digital painter used MidJourney’s default settings and got consistently blurry images. By lowering the CFG scale and adjusting aspect ratios, they doubled the sharpness of their outputs.

❌ Mistake 4: Not Backing Up AI-Generated Work

What Happens? AI platforms can change algorithms, leading to inconsistent results if you rely on single-generation outputs.

Dolores’s Fix:

  • Save multiple versions of AI-generated work.
  • Use cloud storage (e.g., Google Drive, Dropbox) with AI-powered backups.
  • Export files in multiple formats (e.g., PNG, JPEG, PSD).

Example: An NFT artist generated 100 AI characters but only saved the final version. When MidJourney updated its algorithm, their original styles became unrecognizable, forcing a last-minute rework.
