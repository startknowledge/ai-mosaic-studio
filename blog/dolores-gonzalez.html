Chapter 2: 10 Actionable Strategies to Apply Dolores González’s AI Art Techniques

Now that we understand her background, let’s explore practical, step-by-step strategies you can use to integrate AI into your creative workflow—just like Dolores does.

Strategy 1: Start with a Strong Creative Brief (Before Using AI)

Problem: Many artists jump into AI tools without defining their vision, leading to generic, uninspired outputs.

Dolores’ Approach: Before generating anything, she writes a detailed creative brief—a document that includes:

  • The emotional tone (e.g., "dark fantasy," "whimsical," "minimalist")
  • Key visual references (artists, styles, or past works that inspire her)
  • Functional requirements (e.g., "This AI-generated background must integrate with a 3D-rendered character")

How to Apply This:

  1. Define your goal – Are you creating a concept art piece, a marketing graphic, or an NFT collection?
  2. Gather references – Save images, mood boards, or even AI-generated examples that align with your vision.
  3. Use AI as a brainstorming tool – Feed your brief into tools like MidJourney or DALL·E, then refine the output.

Example: A game developer wants an AI-generated medieval fantasy landscape. Instead of just typing "fantasy forest," they’d write:

"A dense, misty forest with towering oak trees, glowing mushrooms, and a hidden ancient ruin. Style references: Alan Lee’s Lord of the Rings illustrations, but with a darker, more surreal twist. Color palette: Deep greens, purples, and gold accents."

This ensures the AI understands the intent rather than producing a generic scene.

Strategy 2: Use AI for Concept Development, Not Final Outputs

Problem: Relying on AI for final art can lead to lifeless, over-processed images that lack originality.

Dolores’ Approach: She treats AI as a concept generator, not a replacement for human refinement. Her workflow:

  1. AI generates 3-5 rough concepts (quick sketches, color palettes, or composition ideas).
  2. She manually refines the best ones into polished final pieces.

How to Apply This:

  • For illustrators: Use AI to brainstorm character designs before sketching.
  • For designers: Let AI generate color schemes (via tools like Adobe Color + AI).
  • For marketers: Use AI to create rough ad concepts, then tweak them in Photoshop.

Example: A children’s book illustrator uses Stable Diffusion to generate 10 different dragon designs based on a brief. Instead of picking one, she combines elements from three different AI outputs to create a unique, hybrid dragon that feels personal.

Strategy 3: Train Custom AI Models for Niche Industries

Problem: Generic AI tools (like MidJourney) work well for broad concepts but struggle with specialized fields (e.g., medical illustrations, architectural visualizations, or fashion design).

Dolores’ Approach: She trains custom AI models using industry-specific datasets to ensure high accuracy and relevance.

How to Apply This:

  1. Collect a dataset – Gather high-quality reference images (e.g., for fashion, use real runway photos).
  2. Use fine-tuning tools – Platforms like Stable Diffusion WebUI or Runway ML allow custom training.
  3. Test and refine – Adjust the model until it matches your brand’s style.

Example: A luxury fashion brand wants AI-generated custom clothing designs. Instead of using a generic AI, they train a custom model on:

  • Past runway looks
  • Textures and fabrics
  • Brand-specific color palettes

This ensures the AI generates designs that align with the brand’s aesthetic—not just random fashion trends.

Strategy 4: Combine AI with Traditional Techniques for Depth

Problem: Pure AI-generated art can look too digital and flat.

Dolores’ Approach: She blends AI with traditional methods—such as:

  • Hand-painting over AI backgrounds
  • Using AI for color correction, then manually adding textures
  • **Generating AI sketches, then refining them in Procreate or Photoshop

How to Apply This:

  • For painters: Use AI to generate a rough sketch, then paint over it.
  • For digital artists: Let AI create a base layer, then add hand-drawn details.
  • For photographers: Use AI to enhance lighting, then manually adjust compositions.

Example: A concept artist uses DALL·E to generate a sci-fi cityscape, then:

  1. Copies the AI image into Photoshop
  2. Hand-paints the buildings to give them a 3D, tactile feel
  3. Adds atmospheric effects (fog, neon lights) manually

The result? A highly detailed, original piece that feels both AI-assisted and handcrafted.

Strategy 5: Use AI for Versioning & Experimentation

Problem: Artists often get stuck in creative ruts because they’re afraid to experiment.

Dolores’ Approach: She uses AI to quickly generate multiple versions of an idea, then picks the best elements from each.

How to Apply This:

  1. Generate 5-10 variations of a concept (e.g., "cyberpunk city at night").
  2. Analyze the differences—which versions have the best lighting? Which compositions feel most dynamic?
  3. Combine the best traits into a final, unique piece.

Example: A game designer wants a new character silhouette. Instead of sketching 20 times, they:

  1. Ask AI for 10 different silhouettes (some with capes, some with armor, some with wings).
  2. Picks the most interesting poses and proportions.
  3. Combines them into a new, original design.

This speeds up iteration while keeping the final result uniquely human.

Strategy 6: Leverage AI for Accessibility & Inclusivity

Problem: Many artists struggle with representing diverse characters, disabilities, or cultural details due to lack of reference material.

Dolores’ Approach: She uses AI to generate inclusive character designs by:

  • Providing specific prompts (e.g., "a wheelchair-using samurai in a cyberpunk setting")
  • Using AI to explore designs that might be culturally sensitive or unfamiliar

How to Apply This:

  • For game developers: Use AI to generate diverse NPCs (non-player characters).
  • For fashion designers: Create AI-generated models of different body types.
  • For educators: Use AI to visualize historical figures accurately.

Example: A storyboard artist needs a character with a rare genetic condition. Instead of guessing, they:

  1. Describe the condition in detail (e.g., "a person with Ehlers-Danlos syndrome in a fantasy setting").
  2. Ask AI for multiple variations.
  3. Refines the most accurate representation with a medical illustrator’s input.

This ensures authenticity and representation in storytelling.

Strategy 7: Automate Repetitive Tasks with AI

Problem: Artists waste time on menial tasks like background removal, color grading, or asset creation.

Dolores’ Approach: She uses AI to automate repetitive work, freeing up time for creative problem-solving.

How to Apply This:

  • Background removal: Use Remove.bg or Adobe Firefly to clean up photos.
  • Color grading: Let AI match a photo’s colors to a mood board (via Photoshop’s AI tools).
  • Asset generation: Use AI to create placeholder textures for 3D models.

Example: A 3D animator needs 100 different rock textures for a game. Instead of manually painting each one:

  1. Uses an AI texture generator (like NVIDIA’s GauGAN2).
  2. Adjusts the prompts to get varied, realistic textures.
  3. Uses them as a starting point, then refines in Substance Painter.

This cuts hours of work into minutes.

Strategy 8: Use AI for Storytelling & Worldbuilding

Problem: Writers and game designers struggle with visualizing entire worlds before development.

Dolores’ Approach: She uses AI to generate immersive environments, creatures, and narratives based on text prompts.

How to Apply This:

  1. Describe a world (e.g., "a floating island civilization with bioluminescent plants").
  2. Ask AI to generate visuals, maps, or even short stories.
  3. Use the outputs as inspiration for your project.

Example: A tabletop RPG designer wants to create a new fantasy race. They:

  1. Write a detailed lore brief (e.g., "a race of tree-dwelling nomads who communicate through song").
  2. Ask AI for visual concepts (clothing, architecture, creatures).
  3. Develop a full campaign setting around these ideas.

This accelerates worldbuilding while keeping it cohesive and engaging.
