1. Start with a Clear Creative Vision Before Using AI

Problem: Many artists jump into AI tools without a defined artistic direction, leading to generic, uninspired outputs.

Dolores González’s Approach: Before generating AI art, González clearly defines the emotional, stylistic, or conceptual goals of her work. For example:

  • "I want to create a series of AI-generated portraits that evoke the surrealism of Magritte."
  • "I need a background texture for a digital illustration that feels like a cyberpunk cityscape."

How to Apply This:

  • Define your artistic intent before using AI.
  • Research styles, artists, or movements that inspire you.
  • Use AI as a collaborator, not a replacement for your vision.

Example: A graphic designer wanted to create AI-generated fashion illustrations. Instead of using a generic AI tool, they first studied 1920s Art Deco fashion and mid-century modern aesthetics. They then fed specific prompts (e.g., "Art Deco dress with geometric patterns, 1925, high contrast") into an AI tool to generate unique, cohesive designs.

2. Use AI for Concept Development, Not Just Final Outputs

Problem: Some artists rely too heavily on AI for final pieces, losing their unique artistic voice.

Dolores González’s Approach: González often uses AI in the early stages of creation—brainstorming, sketching, and refining concepts—before applying her human touch in post-processing.

How to Apply This:

  • Use AI to generate multiple variations of an idea.
  • Select the strongest concepts and refine them manually.
  • Combine AI outputs with traditional techniques (e.g., painting over AI-generated sketches).

Example: A digital artist was stuck on character design. She used an AI tool to generate dozens of facial expressions and body poses, then hand-painted over the best ones to create a unique, cohesive character that felt both AI-assisted and deeply personal.

3. Experiment with AI Style Transfer for Unique Blends

Problem: Many AI art tools produce overly generic or repetitive styles.

Dolores González’s Approach: González combines multiple artistic styles using AI, creating hybrid visual languages. For example, she might take a Renaissance portrait and blend it with cyberpunk neon effects using style transfer techniques.

How to Apply This:

  • Upload reference images of different styles to an AI tool.
  • Experiment with blending prompts (e.g., "Van Gogh’s swirling brushstrokes + futuristic cityscape").
  • Adjust parameters (e.g., temperature, diversity) to control the output.

Example: A street artist wanted to create AI-generated murals that felt like a fusion of graffiti and Renaissance frescoes. They used an AI style transfer tool to layer a graffiti-style base with classical perspective techniques, resulting in visually striking, culturally rich murals.

4. Leverage AI for Automated Backgrounds and Textures

Problem: Creating custom backgrounds and textures can be time-consuming.

Dolores González’s Approach: González uses AI-generated textures to enhance compositions without spending hours on manual design. She often combines AI textures with her own illustrations for depth.

Example: A product designer needed backgrounds for a mobile app’s UI. Instead of manually creating space-themed textures, they used an AI tool to generate galaxy-like patterns, which they then scaled and adjusted for a smooth, professional finish.

Problem: Clients often struggle to visualize ideas before finalizing a design.

Dolores González’s Approach: González generates multiple AI variations of a concept, allowing clients to quickly iterate without extensive manual work.

Problem: Custom typography can be expensive and time-consuming to design.

Problem: Many artists treat AI as a "black box"—they input prompts but don’t understand or refine the outputs.

Dolores González’s Approach: González treats AI as a collaborator, documenting each step and iterating based on feedback.
