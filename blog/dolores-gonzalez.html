8 Actionable Strategies to Apply Dolores González’s AI Art Techniques

If you’re looking to incorporate AI into your artistic practice—whether you're a painter, sculptor, or digital designer—here are eight proven strategies inspired by Dolores’s methodologies.

1. Start with a Strong Foundation in Traditional Art

Why it matters: Dolores emphasizes that AI is a tool, not a replacement for skill. Before diving into AI, mastering composition, color theory, and form ensures that your AI-generated work has depth.

How to apply it:

  • Spend 30-60 minutes daily practicing traditional techniques (sketching, shading, or sculpting).
  • Use AI as a collaborator, not a crutch. For example, if you’re working on a mosaic, train an AI on your personal style before letting it generate variations.
  • Real-world example: A client once asked Dolores to create an AI-generated mural. Instead of feeding the AI generic data, she first hand-painted a reference board—then used AI to expand and refine it, resulting in a hybrid piece that retained her signature brushwork.

2. Train AI on Your Unique Style (Not Just Generic Data)

Most AI art tools rely on publicly available datasets, which can lead to generic, overused styles. Dolores trains her AI on her own work, ensuring that the output remains authentic and distinct.

How to apply it:

  • Curate a personal dataset by scanning or photographing your best traditional pieces.
  • Use platforms like Stable Diffusion’s "LoRA" (Low-Rank Adaptation) to fine-tune AI models to your style.
  • Example: A digital artist used Dolores’s method to train an AI on their watercolor paintings. The result? An AI that could generate new watercolor-style works with the artist’s signature texture and color palette.

3. Use AI for Concept Development, Not Just Execution

Dolores often uses AI in the early stages of a project—not to create the final piece, but to explore ideas, refine concepts, and overcome creative blocks.

How to apply it:

  • Brainstorming with AI: Input vague prompts like "a futuristic city inspired by Renaissance architecture" and let AI generate multiple variations to spark inspiration.
  • Thumbnailing: Use AI to quickly generate composition sketches before committing to a traditional medium.
  • Example: A sculptor working on a public art commission used AI to test different structural designs before finalizing the clay model. This saved weeks of trial and error.

4. Experiment with AI-Assisted Hybrid Techniques

Dolores’s most innovative work comes from combining AI with traditional methods. For instance, she might:

  • Use AI to generate a color palette for a painting.
  • Let AI suggest composition layouts for a mosaic.
  • Train an AI on historical art movements to create new hybrid styles.

How to apply it:

  • Step 1: Create a traditional piece (e.g., a sketch or clay sculpture).
  • Step 2: Use AI to analyze and enhance it (e.g., AI-generated textures, dynamic lighting).
  • Example: A ceramic artist used AI to scan their hand-built vases and then generated digital twins that could be 3D-printed in new shapes—blurring the line between physical and digital craftsmanship.

5. Leverage AI for Real-Time Feedback and Refinement

One of Dolores’s most valuable tools is AI-powered feedback systems that help artists iterate quickly. For example:

  • MidJourney or DALL·E 3 can suggest improvements to a composition.
  • Custom-trained AI models can detect color harmony issues in a painting.

How to apply it:

  • Use AI critique tools (like Artbreeder’s "Style Transfer") to get objective feedback on your work.
  • Example: A painter was struggling with perspective in a landscape piece. By inputting the sketch into an AI, they received real-time suggestions on vanishing points and depth—leading to a more polished final version.

6. Explore Generative Art for Interactive and Dynamic Works

Generative art—where AI creates ever-changing, interactive pieces—is a specialty of Dolores’s. She uses procedural generation to create art that responds to viewer input (e.g., movement, sound, or touch).

How to apply it:

  • Tools to try: Processing (with AI libraries), p5.js, or Unity + ML-Agents.
  • Example: An installation artist created a digital mosaic wall where visitors’ movements triggered AI-generated patterns in real time. The piece evolved differently each time it was viewed.

How to apply it:

Common Mistakes in AI Art (And How to Avoid Them)

Even the most talented artists make mistakes when integrating AI. Here are five pitfalls—and how Dolores avoids them.

1. Over-Reliance on AI Without Human Input

Mistake: Some artists let AI generate the entire piece without human oversight, leading to cold, impersonal work. Solution: Dolores’s rule: "AI should enhance, not replace."

  • Fix: Always review and refine AI outputs manually.
  • Example: A client once asked for a fully AI-generated portrait. Dolores generated 10 variations, then hand-painted the final details to add emotional depth.

2. Using Generic AI Models Without Customization

Mistake: Relying on pre-trained AI models (like DALL·E’s default settings) limits creativity. Solution: Fine-tune AI on your personal style.

  • Fix: Use LoRA (Low-Rank Adaptation) or Stable Diffusion’s "DreamBooth" to train AI on your work.
  • Example: A graffiti artist trained an AI on their street art style, then used it to generate new murals that felt authentically theirs.

3. Ignoring Ethical AI Training Data

Mistake: Some artists use biased or copyrighted datasets, leading to unethical or legally risky work. Solution: Dolores curates her own datasets from public domain or original sources.

  • Fix: Avoid scraping the internet—instead, collect your own reference images.
  • Example: A fashion designer avoided legal issues by training AI on their own sketches rather than using unlicensed runway photos.

4. Not Experimenting with Hybrid Techniques

Mistake: Treating AI as a separate tool rather than a collaborative medium. Solution: Combine traditional and digital methods for unique results.

  • Fix: Try AI-generated textures on physical art or traditional sketches as AI prompts.
  • Example: A sculptor used AI to analyze bone structures, then carved those patterns into marble for a biomorphic sculpture.

5. Failing to Protect Intellectual Property

Mistake: Not documenting or copyrighting AI-assisted work. Solution: Dolores watermarks, timestamps, and registers her AI-generated pieces.

  • Fix: Use blockchain (for NFTs) or traditional copyright registration.
  • Example: An artist used AI to generate a series of digital paintings, then registered them with the U.S. Copyright Office to prevent misuse.

3. Can AI really understand art, or is it just pattern recognition?

Answer: This is a common debate in AI art. Dolores argues that AI doesn’t "understand" art in a human sense, but it excels at recognizing patterns, emotions, and stylistic cues from vast datasets. For example:

  • AI can replicate Van Gogh’s brushstrokes because it’s been trained on thousands of his paintings.
  • However, it lacks true creativity—it combines existing elements rather than innovating like a human artist.

Her approach? Treat AI as a collaborator, not a replacement. She uses it to explore ideas faster, but always refines the final output manually.
