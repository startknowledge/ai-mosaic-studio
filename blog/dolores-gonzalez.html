8 Actionable Strategies to Harness AI Like Dolores González

If you’re an artist, designer, or creative professional looking to integrate AI into your workflow—without losing your artistic voice—here are eight proven strategies inspired by Dolores González’s approach.

1. Treat AI as a Collaborator, Not a Replacement

Problem: Many artists see AI as a tool to generate art for them, leading to generic, impersonal results.

Dolores’ Approach:

  • Start with a concept, not just a prompt.
  • Use AI to refine ideas, not create them from scratch.
  • Blend AI-generated elements with handcrafted details for uniqueness.

How to Apply This:

  • Begin with a rough sketch or mood board.
  • Use AI (like MidJourney or DALL·E) to enhance textures, colors, or compositions.
  • Manually adjust the final piece to add your personal touch.

Example: A painter might use AI to generate background patterns, then paint over them with traditional techniques to maintain authenticity.

2. Experiment with "Prompt Engineering" for Emotional Depth

Problem: Most AI art tools produce visually impressive but emotionally flat images.

Dolores’ Approach:

  • Instead of vague prompts like "a futuristic city", try:
    • "A cyberpunk alleyway where neon signs flicker like dying stars, evoking loneliness and hope."
    • "A surreal landscape where waterfalls flow upward, symbolizing defiance against gravity."

How to Apply This:

  • Describe emotions, not just objects.
  • Use metaphors and storytelling in prompts.
  • Iterate—AI often needs multiple refinements to hit the right tone.

Example: An artist working on a dark fantasy theme might prompt: "A haunted forest at dusk, where the trees whisper forgotten secrets, their bark covered in glowing runes that pulse with an eerie rhythm."

3. Use AI for Concept Development, Not Final Art

Dolores’ Rule: "AI should inspire, not dictate."

How to Apply This:

  • Generate multiple AI variations of a concept.
  • Pick the strongest elements from each version.
  • Combine them manually into a unique final piece.

Example: A graphic designer might:

  1. Use AI to generate 10 different logo concepts for a brand.
  2. Extract the best shapes and colors from each.
  3. Redesign them manually to fit the brand’s identity.

4. Leverage AI for Textures and Backgrounds

Problem: Creating complex textures (like fabric, wood, or metal) can be time-consuming.

Dolores’ Solution:

  • Use AI to generate high-resolution textures in seconds.
  • Blend them seamlessly into your artwork.

Tools to Try:

  • Stable Diffusion (for detailed textures)
  • Topaz Gigapixel AI (for upscaling)
  • Adobe Firefly (for stylized textures)

Example: A digital illustrator might:

  1. Use Stable Diffusion to generate a weathered wooden texture.
  2. Overlay it onto a character’s armor in Photoshop.
  3. Adjust lighting and shadows to make it feel organic.

5. Create AI-Generated "Mood Palettes" for Consistency

Problem: Maintaining color consistency across multiple artworks is difficult.

Dolores’ Trick:

  • Use AI to generate a color palette based on a mood or theme.
  • Apply it uniformly across all your work.

How to Do It:

  1. Prompt AI: "A dark fantasy color palette inspired by gothic architecture and stormy skies."
  2. Extract the colors and use them in Photoshop, Procreate, or Illustrator.
  3. Adjust saturation and contrast for your needs.

Example: A comic artist might:

  1. Generate a palette for a cyberpunk world.
  2. Use the same colors for all backgrounds, characters, and props.
  3. Add variations (like neon highlights) to keep it dynamic.

6. Use AI to Break Creative Blocks

Problem: Artists often face "blank page syndrome" when starting a new project.

Dolores’ Solution:

Prompt Examples:

Common Mistakes in AI Art (And How to Avoid Them)

Even the most enthusiastic AI artists make critical errors that can ruin their work or lead to legal trouble. Here’s what to watch out for—and how Dolores González avoids them.

1. Over-Reliance on AI Without Human Input

Mistake: Using AI to generate entire artworks without any manual intervention.

Why It’s Bad:

  • The art lacks personal style.
  • It’s easily indistinguishable from other AI-generated work.
  • Copyright issues arise if the AI was trained on protected works.

Dolores’ Fix:

  • Always add a human layer—even if it’s just color correction or minor edits.
  • Use AI as a tool, not a replacement.

2. Ignoring Copyright and Ethical Concerns

Mistake: Using AI tools that train on copyrighted art without permission.

Why It’s Bad:

  • Legal risks (e.g., Getty Images vs. Stability AI lawsuit, 2023).
  • Moral dilemmas—are you stealing from artists without credit?

Dolores’ Fix:

  • Use open-source AI models (like Stable Diffusion’s original version).
  • Credit AI tools in your work (e.g., "Generated with MidJourney, refined by [Your Name]").
  • Avoid using AI on protected works (e.g., Disney characters, famous paintings).

3. Vague Prompts Leading to Generic Art

Mistake: Using broad, uninspired prompts like "a beautiful landscape" or "a futuristic city."
