2. The 8 Key Strategies Dolores González Uses to Master AI-Assisted Art

If you’re looking to integrate AI into your creative process—whether you're a digital artist, designer, or marketer—Dolores González’s methodologies provide a roadmap to success. Here are her top 8 strategies:

Strategy 1: Start with a "Creative Prompt Framework" (Not Just Text)

Most AI art tools rely on text prompts, but Dolores argues that vague descriptions lead to vague results. Instead, she uses a structured prompt framework that includes:

  • Subject (What is the main focus?)
  • Style (Is it surrealism, cyberpunk, or impressionist?)
  • Mood (Dark, dreamy, chaotic, serene?)
  • Constraints (Limited color palette, specific textures)
  • Inspiration Sources (Reference images, music, or even a poem)

Example: Instead of: "A futuristic city at night"

Use: "A cyberpunk neon cityscape at 3 AM, inspired by Blade Runner but with a bioluminescent twist. Mood: melancholic yet hopeful. Style: high-contrast, pixel-art influences. Constraints: 90% blues and purples, no human faces visible."

Why It Works: This method reduces ambiguity, ensuring the AI generates art that aligns with your intended narrative.

Strategy 2: Use "Style Transfer Layers" for Seamless Blending

Dolores avoids the "AI art looks like a glitch" problem by using layered style transfer. Instead of applying one AI style at once, she:

  1. Starts with a base image (even a simple sketch).
  2. Applies subtle style influences (e.g., a hint of Van Gogh’s brushstrokes).
  3. Gradually intensifies the effect while maintaining the original composition.

Real-World Example: The "Evolving Portrait" Series In 2023, she worked with a portrait artist to create a series where AI slowly transformed a traditional oil painting into a digital surrealist masterpiece. The process took 12 hours, with the AI learning from the artist’s brushwork patterns before applying its own stylistic tweaks.

Key Takeaway: This technique prevents AI from overpowering the artist’s vision—it enhances it.

Dolores rejects the idea of fully autonomous AI art. Instead, she advocates for a "human-in-the-loop" approach, where:

  1. The AI generates multiple variations (10–20 options).
  2. The artist refines the best candidates (editing, recoloring, or rearranging elements).
  3. The AI learns from feedback and improves future iterations.

Real-World Example: Collaborating with a Muralist A street artist wanted an AI-assisted mural for a festival. Instead of letting the AI design the entire piece, Dolores:

Common Mistakes in AI-Assisted Art (And How to Avoid Them)

Even with AI tools, many artists and designers make critical errors that undermine their work. Here’s how to steer clear of them:

Mistake 1: Treating AI as a "Magic Button"

Problem: Some users input a prompt and expect a masterpiece without any refinement. Solution:

  • Always review and edit AI-generated art.
  • Use AI for concept development, not final output.

Dolores’s Tip: "AI is your collaborator, not your replacement. Treat it like a sketch artist—great ideas come from iteration."

Mistake 2: Ignoring Copyright and Ethics

Problem: Using AI tools that steal from artists without compensation. Solution:

  • Choose ethical AI platforms (like AI Mosaic Studio, which uses licensed datasets).
  • Credit AI contributions transparently.

Dolores’s Case Study: A popular AI art generator was sued for unauthorized use of 500+ artists’ styles. Dolores helped negotiate a settlement where the company:

  • Paid royalties to affected artists.
  • Implemented opt-out mechanisms for artists who didn’t want their styles used.

Mistake 3: Over-Reliance on AI for "Unique" Work

Problem: AI generates similar outputs for similar prompts, leading to generic art. Solution:

  • Combine multiple AI tools (e.g., use one for composition, another for color grading).
  • Add manual elements to make work distinct.

Example: Two artists used the same prompt ("a cyberpunk robot in a rainstorm"). One relied 100% on AI—the result looked generic. The other sketched the robot first, then used AI to enhance textures and lighting—the final piece was uniquely theirs.

Mistake 4: Not Testing AI with Real-World Constraints

Problem: AI works well in controlled environments but fails when applied to real projects (e.g., deadlines, client feedback). Solution:

  • Simulate real conditions before finalizing AI-generated work.
  • Have a "fallback plan" (e.g., traditional tools if AI fails).

Dolores’s Advice: "Always test AI outputs with real users—not just yourself. If a client doesn’t ‘get’ it, the AI didn’t do its job."

Mistake 5: Neglecting the "Human Touch"

Problem: AI can replicate styles, but it can’t replicate emotion. Solution:

  • Use AI to enhance, not replace, emotional expression.
  • Edit AI work to reflect your personal voice.

Example: An AI-generated portrait looked flawless technically but lacked soul. Dolores’s solution?

  • The artist hand-painted the eyes—the only human element—which transformed the piece from cold to warm.
