How Dolores González Uses AI in Her Work: A Deep Dive

1. AI-Powered Generative Mosaics

One of Dolores González’s most innovative contributions is her work with AI-generated mosaics. Unlike traditional mosaics, which require manual tile selection and placement, her AI-driven approach uses neural networks to generate patterns that adapt to space, color, and cultural context.

How It Works:

  • Input: A base image (e.g., a landscape, portrait, or abstract shape).
  • AI Processing: A GAN (Generative Adversarial Network) or VAE (Variational Autoencoder) analyzes the image and generates thousands of possible mosaic variations.
  • Output: A unique, high-resolution mosaic that maintains the original’s essence while introducing new artistic layers.

Real-World Example: The "Neural Tiles" Project

In 2022, González collaborated with a public art initiative to create an AI-generated mosaic wall in a city plaza. Instead of using pre-made tiles, she trained an AI on historical mosaic patterns from the region, then let the algorithm generate a new design that paid homage to local heritage while incorporating modern digital aesthetics.

The result was a 100-square-meter wall that changed slightly every few hours, adapting to time of day, weather, and viewer interaction via a mobile app. This project demonstrated how AI can make art dynamic, interactive, and ever-evolving.

2. AI-Assisted Color Theory & Palette Generation

Color is one of the most psychologically impactful elements in art. González uses AI to automate color palette selection, ensuring that her work emotionally resonates with viewers.

How It Works:

  • Input: A reference image or mood board.
  • AI Processing: Tools like Google’s Magenta or Runway ML analyze color harmony, contrast, and cultural associations.
  • Output: A custom color palette optimized for visual appeal and emotional impact.

Real-World Example: The "Emotion Palettes" Collection

González developed a series of AI-generated color palettes tied to human emotions (e.g., "Serenity," "Joy," "Melancholy"). Instead of relying on predefined schemes, she trained an AI on art history, psychology studies, and cultural symbolism to create nuanced, context-aware palettes.

Artists and designers now use these palettes in digital illustrations, branding, and even fashion, proving that AI can enhance—not replace—human creativity.

8 Actionable Strategies to Apply Dolores González’s AI Techniques

Now that we’ve explored González’s methods, let’s break down how you can implement them in your own work—whether you're an artist, designer, or tech enthusiast.

Strategy 1: Start with a Strong Foundation in Traditional Art

González’s work suggests that AI is most powerful when paired with human skill. Before diving into AI tools, develop a strong foundation in:

  • Drawing & composition (understanding form, light, and space).
  • Color theory (how colors interact and evoke emotions).
  • Digital tools (Photoshop, Procreate, or Blender for 3D).

Why? AI can enhance your work, but it can’t replace fundamental artistic knowledge.

Action Step:

  • Spend 30 minutes daily practicing traditional sketching before experimenting with AI.
  • Study art history to understand how past techniques can inform AI-generated art.

Strategy 2: Experiment with AI Mosaic Generation

If you’re interested in AI mosaics, follow these steps:

  1. Choose a base image (e.g., a photograph, painting, or abstract shape).
  2. Use an AI mosaic generator (tools like DeepMosaic, AI Mosaic Generator, or custom Python scripts).
  3. Adjust parameters (tile size, color palette, randomness level).
  4. Refine manually (crop, recolor, or add details).

Real-World Tip: González often trains her AI on specific datasets (e.g., Roman mosaics, Islamic patterns) to ensure cultural authenticity. You can do this by:

  • Collecting high-quality reference images.
  • Using TensorFlow or PyTorch to fine-tune a model.

Strategy 3: Use AI for Color Palette Generation

Instead of guessing colors, let AI suggest palettes based on emotion, culture, or context.

Tools to Try:

  • Adobe Color (AI-assisted palette generator).
  • Coolors (random but harmonious palettes).
  • Paletton (advanced color theory tools).

Action Step:

  • Upload a reference image (e.g., a sunset, a portrait).
  • Let AI generate 5-10 palette options.
  • Pick the one that best matches your vision.

Pro Tip: González often combines AI-generated palettes with manual adjustments to ensure emotional resonance.

Strategy 4: Automate Concept Development with AI

Before committing to a project, use AI to explore multiple directions.

How to Do It:

  1. Describe your idea in detail (e.g., "a cyberpunk city at night").
  2. Use MidJourney/DALL·E to generate 10-20 variations.
  3. Select the most promising ones and refine them.

Example Workflow:

  • Input: "A futuristic marketplace with neon signs and holograms."
  • AI Output: 15 different interpretations.
  • Your Choice: Pick the one with best lighting and composition.

Strategy 5: Train Your Own AI Model (For Advanced Users)

If you want full control, train a custom AI model for your specific needs.

Steps:

  1. Gather a dataset (e.g., 1000+ images of your preferred style).
  2. Use a framework like Stable Diffusion, GANs, or Runway ML.
  3. Fine-tune the model to match your artistic vision.
  4. Generate new art based on your trained model.

Warning: This requires technical knowledge, but the results can be uniquely tailored to your style.

Strategy 6: Collaborate with AI for Hybrid Art

Don’t treat AI as a replacement—treat it as a collaborator.

How to Collaborate:

  • Start with AI-generated sketches → refine them manually.
  • Use AI for music/soundscapes → compose the final track.
  • Let AI generate text prompts → expand them into a story.

Example: González once used AI to generate a poem, then rewrote it in her own voice to make it more personal.

Strategy 7: Document & Iterate

AI art is not a one-time process—it’s about continuous refinement.

Best Practices:

  • Keep a digital sketchbook of AI-generated ideas.
  • Test multiple variations before finalizing.
  • Share work-in-progress for feedback.

González’s Approach: She often posts "AI experiments" on social media, getting real-time feedback from the community.

Strategy 8: Stay Ethical & Transparent

As AI art grows, ethical concerns (like misuse of AI, plagiarism, and copyright) become critical.

How to Stay Ethical:

  • Credit AI tools when appropriate.
  • Avoid deepfakes or misleading AI-generated content.
  • Respect copyright (don’t train AI on others’ work without permission).

González’s Stance: She believes artists should own their AI tools and communicate transparently about how AI was used.

Common Mistakes in AI-Assisted Art (And How to Avoid Them)

Even the most talented artists make mistakes when using AI. Here are five pitfalls and how to avoid them.

Mistake 1: Relying Too Much on AI Without Human Input

Problem: Some artists let AI generate everything, leading to generic, uninspired work.

Solution:

  • Use AI as a starting point, not the final product.
  • Add your personal touch (e.g., hand-drawn details, unique textures).

González’s Advice: "AI can suggest a thousand ideas, but only you can decide which one feels right."

Mistake 2: Ignoring Copyright & Fair Use

Problem: Some artists train AI on copyrighted material without permission, leading to legal issues.

Solution:

  • Use public domain or licensed datasets.
  • Avoid scraping art from the internet without consent.

Legal Tip: Check Creative Commons licenses before using AI training data.

Mistake 3: Overcomplicating the Process

Problem: Trying to master every AI tool at once can be overwhelming.

Solution:

  • Start with one tool (e.g., MidJourney for prompts, Photoshop for editing).
  • Master the basics before exploring advanced techniques.

González’s Workflow: She often begins with simple AI tools, then gradually incorporates more complex ones.

Mistake 4: Not Experimenting Enough

Problem: Some artists stick to one AI style, missing out on new possibilities.

Solution:

  • Try different AI models (e.g., DALL·E, Stable Diffusion, Runway ML).
  • Experiment with prompts (e.g., "cyberpunk city" vs. "medieval fantasy").

Creative Challenge: Pick three random AI-generated images and combine them into one new piece.

Mistake 5: Forgetting the "Why" Behind the Art

Problem: Some AI art feels empty or meaningless because it lacks emotional depth.

Solution:

  • Ask yourself: "What story does this piece tell?"
  • Connect AI-generated elements to personal experiences.

González’s Philosophy: "The best AI art isn’t just pretty—it’s meaningful."
