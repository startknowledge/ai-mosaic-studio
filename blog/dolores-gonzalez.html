Most AI art platforms rely on pre-trained models (like DALL·E or MidJourney), which limit creativity. González’s approach? Fine-tuning AI with your own data.

How to do it:

  • Collect 100+ high-quality images that align with your artistic style.

Real-World Example: A street artist named Marco wanted to create AI-generated murals. Instead of using MidJourney, he trained a model on his own sketchbook drawings and urban photography. The result? Common Mistakes in AI-Generated Art (And How to Avoid Them)

Even the most talented artists make mistakes when working with AI. Here are five pitfalls—and how to prevent them—based on Dolores González’s insights.

1. Over-Reliance on Generic AI Prompts

Mistake: Using vague or cliché prompts like "a beautiful landscape" or "a cute cat." Why it fails: AI generates mediocre, predictable results when given no direction.

Solution:

  • Be specific: "A cyberpunk city at sunset with neon reflections on rain-soaked streets."
  • Use emotional adjectives: "A portrait that feels like melancholic nostalgia."
  • Experiment with unusual combinations: "A steampunk robot made of coral and brass."

González’s Tip:

"The more unique and vivid your prompt, the more surprising your AI output will be."

2. Ignoring the "Seed" or Randomness in AI Generation

Mistake: Assuming every AI generation will be identical if given the same prompt. Why it fails: AI uses randomness (seeds) to vary outputs. Without controlling this, you get inconsistent results.

Solution:

  • Set a fixed seed (if your AI tool allows it) for reproducible results.
  • Run multiple generations and compare them to find the best one.
  • Use "variations" mode to explore slight differences.

Real-World Example: An artist named Ryan was frustrated because his AI-generated characters looked different every time. He then fixed the seed and adjusted the prompt slightly to get consistent but varied results.

3. Neglecting Post-Processing

Mistake: Assuming AI output is perfect as-is and skipping editing. Why it fails: AI can misinterpret prompts, leading to unbalanced compositions or odd colors.

Solution:

  • Use Photoshop/Lightroom to adjust lighting, colors, and sharpness.
  • Apply selective edits (e.g., darkening shadows, brightening highlights).
  • Use AI tools for refinement (e.g., Topaz Gigapixel for upscaling).

González’s Tip:

"AI is your first draft—your editing is where the magic happens."

4. Not Experimenting with Different AI Models

Mistake: Sticking to one AI tool (e.g., MidJourney or DALL·E) without exploring others. Why it fails: Each AI has different strengths—some are better for realism, others for abstract art.

Solution:

  • Test multiple AI platforms (e.g., Stable Diffusion, Leonardo.AI, Runway ML).
  • Compare results and pick the best for your project.
  • Combine models (e.g., use DALL·E for concepts and Stable Diffusion for refinement).

Real-World Example: A concept artist named Alex tried MidJourney for fantasy scenes but found Stable Diffusion better for detailed textures. By switching tools, he doubled his productivity.

5. Forgetting About Copyright and Ethics

Mistake: Using AI-generated art without understanding copyright laws. Why it fails: Some AI models are trained on copyrighted material, leading to legal risks.

Solution:

  • Use open-source AI models (e.g., Stable Diffusion’s official version).
  • Avoid generating art based on protected IP (e.g., Disney characters, real people without consent).
  • Check AI platform terms before using them commercially.

González’s Ethical Stance:

"AI art should inspire, not exploit. Always credit sources and respect intellectual property."
