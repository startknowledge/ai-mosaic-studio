1. Start with a "Creative DNA" Framework

Dolores believes that great AI art begins with understanding the "essence" of what you’re creating. Before jumping into AI tools, she recommends defining:

  • The emotional core (e.g., "nostalgic," "futuristic," "whimsical").
  • The cultural context (e.g., "Latin American folklore," "cyberpunk aesthetics").
  • The functional purpose (e.g., "brand identity," "NFT collection," "personal memoir").

How to Apply This:

  • Step 1: Ask yourself: What single word describes the "vibe" of my project?
  • Step 2: Use this word as a base prompt in AI tools (e.g., "A cyberpunk cityscape with a neon-lit plaza, evoking a sense of 'hope in decay'").
  • Step 3: Refine with adjective pairs (e.g., "darkly luminous," "chaotic harmony").

Real-World Example: A client wanted an AI-generated album cover for a singer-songwriter blending tropicalia and electronic music. Dolores helped them define the "Creative DNA" as:

  • Emotional Core: "Joyful melancholy"
  • Cultural Context: "Brazilian 1970s counterculture"
  • Functional Purpose: "Aesthetic that feels like a live performance"

The result? A series of 12 AI artworks that evolved from sun-drenched carnival scenes to glitchy, psychedelic soundwave visuals—each piece feeling like a snapshot of a moment in the artist’s journey.

2. Use "Prompt Engineering" Like a Storyteller

Most people treat AI prompts like shopping lists ("give me a cat in a hat"). Dolores treats them like short stories—rich in imagery, mood, and subtext.

Her Prompt Formula:

[Subject] + [Style] + [Mood] + [Unique Detail] + [Cultural/Historical Reference]

Example Prompt Breakdown: *"A 19th-century Mexican calavera skeleton dancing in a neon-lit cyberpunk nightclub, with Day of the Dead marigolds glowing like holograms, painted in the style of Frida Kahlo meets Zdzisław Beksiński, evoking a sense of 'defiant joy in the face of oblivion.'"

Pro Tips:

  • Avoid vague terms like "beautiful" or "modern." Instead, use specific emotions ("hauntingly beautiful").
  • Incorporate contradictions (e.g., "a fragile castle made of glass and steel") to force the AI to think creatively.
  • Use "emotional triggers" like "a sense of longing," "a moment of revelation," or "the weight of history."

Real-World Example: A luxury fashion brand wanted AI-generated illustrations for a campaign about "sustainable elegance." Dolores crafted prompts like: *"A silk scarf unraveling into a forest of regenerating trees, woven in the style of Albrecht Dürer’s engravings but with a modern, minimalist edge, evoking 'transformation through decay.'"

The results were highly shareable, Instagram-worthy artworks that aligned perfectly with the brand’s messaging.

6. Leverage AI for "Reverse Engineering" Existing Art

Instead of starting from scratch, Dolores uses AI to analyze and reinterpret existing artworks. This is useful for:

  • Redesigning old logos with a modern twist.
  • Creating "homages" to classic artists (e.g., "a Van Gogh painting but in a cyberpunk style").
  • Generating "lost" art (e.g., "what if Picasso painted in 3D?").

How She Does It:

  1. Uploads a reference image (e.g., a Monet painting).
  2. Uses AI to extract "style tokens" (colors, brushstrokes, composition).
  3. Applies these tokens to a new subject (e.g., "a modern cityscape in Monet’s style").

Real-World Example: A wine brand wanted to modernize their vintage label designs. Dolores:

  1. Analyzed 10+ classic wine labels from the 1920s.
  2. Extracted the "vintage aesthetic" (e.g., antique fonts, sepia tones, ornate borders).
