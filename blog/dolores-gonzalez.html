8 Actionable Strategies to Master AI Art Like Dolores González

If you’re looking to elevate your AI-generated art—whether for personal projects, client work, or business branding—here are eight proven strategies inspired by González’s techniques.

1. Start with a Strong Concept Before Generating AI Art

Dolores González’s approach begins long before pressing "Generate." She emphasizes that the best AI art is not just random outputs but intentional creations.

How to Apply This:

  • Define your artistic vision before using AI tools.
  • Sketch rough concepts (even if you’re not a traditional artist).
  • Use AI as a collaborator, not a replacement for human creativity.

Real-World Example: A digital artist using AI Mosaic Studio wanted to create a cyberpunk-inspired cityscape. Instead of relying solely on AI, they:

  1. Drafted a rough wireframe of the scene.
  2. Defined key elements (neon lights, futuristic architecture, rain effects).
  3. Used AI to refine textures and details, ensuring the final piece aligned with their original concept.

Result? A highly cohesive, visually striking piece that balanced human imagination with AI precision.

2. Experiment with Procedural Generation for Unique Patterns

Procedural generation is a core principle in González’s work—using algorithms to create infinite variations of a theme. This is especially useful for AI mosaics, textures, and dynamic backgrounds.

How to Apply This:

  • Use AI tools with procedural controls (e.g., adjusting seed values, noise parameters).
  • Test different algorithms to see which produces the most interesting results.
  • Combine multiple AI-generated elements to build complex compositions.

Real-World Example: A game developer working on an open-world RPG needed procedurally generated terrain. They used Dolores González-inspired techniques by:

  • Setting base parameters (mountain heights, river paths, vegetation density).
  • Letting AI refine textures while keeping structural integrity.
  • Manually tweaking outliers to ensure the environment felt organic, not robotic.

Result? A seamless, ever-changing landscape that kept players engaged without requiring manual design for every patch of land.

3. Use AI to Enhance, Not Replace, Your Existing Work

One of González’s key insights is that AI should augment, not replace, human creativity. Many artists fall into the trap of over-relying on AI, leading to generic, soulless designs.

How to Apply This:

  • Start with a base design, then use AI to enhance details (e.g., textures, lighting, color grading).
  • Blend AI-generated elements with hand-drawn or photographed assets.
  • Use AI for repetitive tasks (e.g., background generation, pattern tiling) while keeping key elements human-made.

Real-World Example: A book cover designer struggled with consistently creating unique, eye-catching covers. They adopted González’s hybrid approach by:

  1. Designing a rough cover layout in Photoshop.
  2. Using AI to generate complementary textures (e.g., abstract patterns, grunge effects).
  3. Manually adjusting typography and focal points to ensure the cover felt authentic and marketable.

Result? A portfolio of covers that stood out in a saturated market, with AI handling the heavy lifting while the designer retained creative control.

4. Leverage AI for Mood Board Creation

Before finalizing an AI-generated piece, González recommends building mood boards—visual references that guide the AI’s output.

How to Apply This:

  • Collect images, colors, and styles that inspire your project.
  • Use AI tools to analyze trends in your mood board (e.g., dominant colors, common themes).
  • Refine AI prompts based on these insights.

Real-World Example: A branding agency needed to create a new identity for a wellness startup. They:

  1. Curated a mood board with organic shapes, soft pastels, and nature-inspired elements.
  2. Used AI to generate logo variations based on these references.
  3. Selected the strongest concepts and iterated with human input to refine the final design.

Result? A cohesive, emotionally resonant brand that aligned perfectly with the client’s vision.

5. Optimize AI Prompts for Better Results

The quality of your AI-generated art depends 80% on the prompt. Dolores González’s research shows that specific, descriptive prompts yield far superior results than vague ones.

How to Apply This:

  • Be detailed (e.g., "A cyberpunk city at night, neon signs reflecting on wet pavement, rain effects, ultra HD, cinematic lighting").
  • Use adjectives to guide the AI’s style ("vibrant," "minimalist," "surreal," "retro-futuristic").
  • Avoid generic terms like "art" or "design"—instead, specify medium, mood, and context.

Real-World Example: A filmmaker wanted AI-generated concept art for a sci-fi film. They initially tried:

  • "A spaceship" → Generic, uninspired result. Then they refined their prompt to:
  • "A sleek, futuristic starship, metallic hull with glowing energy veins, hyper-detailed cockpit interior, cyberpunk aesthetic, ultra HD, cinematic lighting, inspired by Blade Runner 2049 and Ghost in the Shell."

Result? A visually stunning, production-ready piece that exceeded expectations.

González’s work extends beyond static images—she explores interactive AI art, where user input changes the output in real time.

How to Apply This:

One of González’s most valuable insights is that AI-generated art isn’t just for one medium—it can be adapted for web, print, merchandise, and more.

How to Apply This:

8. Iterate and Refine—AI Art Is a Work in Progress

González emphasizes that AI art is rarely "done" in one try. The best results come from multiple iterations, tweaking parameters, and human oversight.

How to Apply This:

  • Generate multiple variations of your idea.
  • Compare outputs and identify what works and what doesn’t.
  • Adjust prompts or settings based on feedback.
  • Combine the best elements from different AI runs.

Real-World Example: A graphic designer was tasked with creating a series of AI-generated illustrations for a children’s book. They:

Common Mistakes in AI Art (And How to Avoid Them)

Even the most talented artists can fall into pitfalls when working with AI. Here are five major mistakes and how to prevent them:

❌ Mistake 1: Over-Reliance on AI Without Human Input

Problem: Some creators let AI make all decisions, leading to generic, soulless designs. Solution:

  • Always review and refine AI outputs.
  • Use AI as a tool, not a replacement for creativity.

❌ Mistake 2: Using Vague Prompts

Problem: Generic prompts like "Make me a cool picture" lead to unpredictable, unsatisfying results. Solution:

  • Be specific—describe style, mood, and details.
  • Use reference images to guide the AI.

❌ Mistake 3: Ignoring Ethical Considerations

Problem: Some AI art tools lack transparency about training data, leading to unintended biases or copyright issues. Solution:

  • Research AI tools for ethical sourcing.
  • Credit original artists when using AI-generated work.

❌ Mistake 4: Not Experimenting Enough

Problem: Many artists stick to one AI tool or style, missing out on new possibilities. Solution:

  • Try different AI platforms (e.g., MidJourney, DALL·E, AI Mosaic Studio).
  • Test new techniques like procedural generation and dynamic art.

❌ Mistake 5: Skipping Post-Processing

Problem: AI-generated art often needs color correction, sharpening, or manual touches to look polished. Solution:

  • Use Photoshop, Procreate, or GIMP to refine AI outputs.
  • Add textures, shadows, or effects for a professional finish.
