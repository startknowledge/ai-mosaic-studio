8 Actionable Strategies for AI-Assisted Art (Inspired by Dolores González)

Dolores González’s approach to AI art is not about replacing human creativity but amplifying it. Below are eight proven strategies she uses to integrate AI into her workflow—techniques you can adapt for your own projects.

1. Start with a Strong Concept Before AI Intervention

Why it matters: AI excels at pattern recognition and style replication, but it struggles with abstract ideas. González’s first step is always defining a clear artistic vision before feeding prompts into AI tools.

How she does it:

  • She begins with sketches or mood boards to outline the theme, color palette, and emotional tone.
  • For example, when creating an AI-generated mural for a corporate space, she first sketches the composition and key elements (e.g., futuristic cityscapes with organic textures) before using AI to refine details.

Actionable tip: ✅ Before using AI, ask yourself:

  • What is the core message of my artwork?
  • What emotional response do I want to evoke?
  • What traditional techniques can I combine with AI?

Real-world example: González once worked on a project for a sustainability nonprofit. Instead of letting AI generate random nature scenes, she first researched the client’s mission—focusing on deforestation and reforestation. She then used AI to enhance her hand-drawn sketches of trees, adding hyper-detailed foliage while maintaining a minimalist, impactful style.

2. Use AI for Concept Development, Not Just Execution

Why it matters: Many artists treat AI as a finishing tool, but González sees it as a collaborative brainstorming partner.

How she does it:

  • She inputs vague prompts (e.g., "a cyberpunk forest with neon blue leaves") to generate multiple AI variations.
  • She then selects the strongest concepts and refines them manually before finalizing.

Actionable tip: ✅ Try "AI sketching" for brainstorming:

  • Use tools like MidJourney or DALL·E 3 to generate 10-20 variations of a single idea.
  • Pick the most intriguing and build upon it with traditional media.

Real-world example: For a fashion brand’s AI-generated collection, González started with a prompt: "Victorian-era dresses with holographic fabrics." The AI produced dozens of styles, but she focused on three key designs—each with a different color scheme and texture. She then hand-painted the final details, ensuring the AI’s suggestions aligned with the brand’s aesthetic.

3. Combine AI with Traditional Techniques for Unique Textures

Why it matters: AI can generate stunning visuals, but textural depth often requires human touch. González blends AI-generated elements with physical media (e.g., watercolors, ink, or mosaic tiles) for unmatched richness.

How she does it:

  • She uses AI to create digital patterns (e.g., fractals, abstract shapes).
  • She then prints these patterns onto fabric or paper and applies them to physical artworks.
  • For mosaics, she trains AI on historical tile patterns and uses the output as a blueprint for manual tile placement.

Actionable tip: ✅ Experiment with "AI + physical media" hybrids:

  • Generate an AI background, then paint over it with acrylics.
  • Use AI to design a mosaic grid, then cut and arrange real tiles based on the AI’s suggestions.

Real-world example: In a public art installation, González used AI to simulate ancient Greek mosaic patterns. She then manually arranged ceramic tiles following the AI’s suggested layout, resulting in a hybrid digital-physical masterpiece that preserved historical accuracy while incorporating modern innovation.

4. Train AI on Your Own Art Style for Consistent Output

Why it matters: Generic AI tools often produce generic results. González fine-tunes AI models using her own artwork to ensure consistency in style.

How she does it:

  • She uploads 10-20 of her best pieces to AI training platforms (e.g., Stable Diffusion’s custom training).
  • She then uses style references when generating new art, ensuring the AI mimics her unique brushstrokes and color palette.

Actionable tip: ✅ Create a "style library" for AI:

  • Save 5-10 of your strongest pieces in a folder.
  • Use MidJourney’s --style parameter or DALL·E’s "reference image" feature to guide AI output.

Real-world example: A luxury watch brand hired González to create AI-generated watch dials that matched their vintage aesthetic. By training AI on her retro-inspired illustrations, she ensured every dial had the same nostalgic charm, even when generated at scale.

5. Use AI for Rapid Prototyping and Client Feedback

Why it matters: Clients often struggle to visualize abstract ideas. González uses AI to quickly generate prototypes, making the creative process faster and more collaborative.

How she does it:

  • She creates multiple AI variations of a client’s concept.
  • She shares these with the client for early feedback before committing to a final design.
  • This reduces revisions and ensures alignment with the client’s vision.

Actionable tip: ✅ Implement "AI sprints" for client projects:

  • Generate 3-5 AI concepts in one session.
  • Get client input before investing time in manual refinement.

Real-world example: For a branding project, a client wanted a logo that blended futuristic and organic elements. González used AI to generate 12 logo variations in under an hour. The client selected one, and she refined it manually, resulting in a final design approved in two days—instead of the usual two weeks.

6. Leverage AI for Automated Color Palette Generation

Why it matters: Color psychology is critical in art, but finding the perfect palette can be time-consuming. González uses AI to analyze trends, moods, and cultural references to suggest harmonious color schemes.

How she does it:

  • She inputs keywords (e.g., "medieval castle," "cyberpunk dystopia") into AI color palette generators.
  • She then adjusts the AI’s suggestions to match her personal aesthetic.

Actionable tip: ✅ Use AI for "emotional color mapping":

  • Try tools like Adobe Color’s AI suggestions or Coolors’ AI generator.
  • Combine AI palettes with traditional color theory for balance.

Real-world example: For a children’s book illustration, González needed a whimsical yet balanced color scheme. She used AI to generate fantasy-inspired palettes, then manually tweaked the shades to ensure high contrast and readability for young readers.

7. Apply AI to Restore and Enhance Historical Art

Why it matters: Many old masterpieces are damaged or faded. González uses AI to digitally restore artworks while preserving their historical integrity.

How she does it:

  • She scans damaged paintings or mosaics and uses AI restoration tools (e.g., Google’s Art Restoration AI).

Actionable tip: ✅ Explore AI for "digital archaeology":

  • Use DeepImagePrior or NVIDIA’s MoMA Artwork Analysis Tool to fill in missing sections of old art.

Real-world example: A 16th-century fresco in a Spanish monastery had large sections of peeling paint. González used AI to reconstruct the missing areas based on the remaining visible patterns. The restoration was approved by art historians, proving AI’s potential in cultural preservation.

  1. Free samples (for portfolios or social media).
  2. Limited-edition prints (for collectors).
  3. Custom commissions (for clients with specific needs).

Common Mistakes in AI-Assisted Art (And How to Avoid Them)

Even the most talented artists make pitfalls when integrating AI. Here are five frequent errors and how Dolores González avoids them:

1. Over-Reliance on AI Without Human Touch

Mistake: Some artists let AI generate entire pieces without manual intervention, leading to generic, soulless art.

Solution:

  • Always refine AI output with traditional techniques.
  • Add imperfections (e.g., hand-drawn textures, physical media) to humanize the AI-generated work.

González’s approach: She never uses AI as a standalone tool—instead, she sees it as a collaborator, not a replacement.

2. Ignoring Copyright and Ethical Concerns

Mistake: Some AI artists don’t credit their sources, leading to legal disputes or plagiarism accusations.

Solution:

  • Use original references (your own art, public domain images).
  • Acknowledge AI tools used in the process.
  • Avoid training AI on copyrighted material without permission.

González’s approach: She only trains AI on her own work or public domain art, ensuring full ethical compliance.

3. Skipping the Research Phase

Mistake: Jumping straight into AI generation without understanding the subject leads to inaccurate or off-brand art.

Solution:

  • Research the topic deeply before generating AI art.
  • Use AI as a tool, not a shortcut.

González’s approach: For a historical mosaic project, she studied ancient techniques before using AI to simulate patterns.

4. Neglecting SEO and Digital Marketing for AI Art

Mistake: Many AI artists don’t promote their work, limiting their reach.

Solution:

  • Optimize AI-generated art for search engines (e.g., descriptive filenames, alt text).
  • Use social media to showcase the AI-human collaboration process.
  • Offer free samples to build a portfolio.

González’s approach: She blogs about her AI art process, shares tutorials, and engages with art communities to drive traffic to her studio.

5. Underpricing AI-Assisted Work

Mistake: Treating AI art as "free or cheap" because it’s "just code."

Solution:

  • Charge for the time, skill, and creativity involved.
  • Offer tiered pricing (e.g., basic AI generation vs. full customization).

González’s approach: She positions AI as a premium service, charging higher rates for hybrid (AI + human) projects.
