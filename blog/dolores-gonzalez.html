Chapter 2: How Dolores González Blends AI with Human Creativity

González’s approach to AI art is not about letting the algorithm take full control—it’s about directing the machine while allowing it to surprise you. Here’s how she does it:

1. The "Prompt Engineering" Method

Unlike generic AI art tools where users input vague descriptions, González uses hyper-specific, emotionally charged prompts to guide AI generation. For example:

  • Instead of "a futuristic city," she might say:

    "A neon-lit dystopian metropolis where the skyscrapers are made of liquid memory, reflecting the fragmented dreams of a lone artist who once believed in utopia. The colors should shift between electric blue and sickly green, with a subtle glow of forgotten hope."

This method ensures the AI doesn’t just generate a generic image but one that aligns with her artistic vision.

2. Iterative Refinement (The "AI Sketchbook" Approach)

González doesn’t expect AI to produce a final piece in one go. Instead, she:

  1. Generates multiple rough drafts (often 20-30 variations).
  2. Analyzes the "DNA" of each—looking for patterns, moods, and unexpected elements.
  3. Refines the prompt based on what resonates most.
  4. Combines elements from different generations to create a unique hybrid piece.

This process is similar to how traditional artists work with sketches and underdrawings—except the "sketches" are generated by AI.

3. Emotional Anchoring (The "Human Filter")

Even with AI’s ability to generate visually stunning work, González insists on human emotional input. She uses:

  • Mood boards (physical or digital) to define the vibe of a piece.
  • Soundscapes (playing music or ambient noise) while generating art to influence the AI’s output.
  • Biometric feedback (via wearables) to adjust AI parameters based on heart rate and skin conductance.

Example: For her "Neural Dreams" series, she had participants wear EEG headbands while they slept. The AI then interpreted their brainwave patterns into visual art, creating pieces that felt deeply personal yet universally relatable.

4. The "Collaborative Canvas" Technique

González often works with multiple AI models simultaneously, letting them compete and collaborate in real time. For instance:

  • She might run one AI model generating abstract shapes while another refines color palettes.
  • A third model could compose the background, and a fourth adds subtle textures.
  • Finally, she manually tweaks the final piece to ensure it feels intentionally human.

This method mimics how traditional artists layer techniques (e.g., oil painting with underpainting).

Chapter 3: 8 Actionable Strategies to Apply AI in Your Art (Like Dolores González)

If you’re an artist, designer, or creative looking to incorporate AI into your workflow, Dolores González’s methods offer practical, step-by-step strategies. Here’s how you can apply them:

Strategy 1: Start with a "Creative Brief" (Not Just a Prompt)

González never jumps into AI generation without a clear artistic brief. Before using any AI tool, ask yourself:

  • What emotion or message do I want to convey?
  • What are the key visual elements? (e.g., colors, textures, composition)
  • What’s the "vibe" I’m going for? (e.g., melancholic, futuristic, nostalgic)

Example: If you’re creating a cyberpunk cityscape, your brief might include: ✅ Colors: Neon pinks, deep purples, electric blues ✅ Elements: Floating holograms, rain-soaked streets, towering megastructures ✅ Mood: Paranoid yet hopeful

Then, translate this into a detailed prompt for your AI tool.

Strategy 2: Use AI for "Brainstorming," Not Final Outputs

González treats AI as a creative brainstorming partner, not a replacement for her own skill. She generates dozens of variations to:

  • Find unexpected angles she wouldn’t have considered.
  • Test color palettes and compositions quickly.
  • Get inspiration for traditional techniques.

Action Step:

  • Use AI to generate 10-20 rough concepts for a project.
  • Pick the most intriguing ones and refine them manually.
  • Combine elements from different AI outputs to create something new and unique.

Strategy 3: Layer AI with Traditional Techniques

González often starts with AI-generated elements and then enhances them with hand-drawn or physical media. For example:

  • She might use AI to generate a background, then paint over it with acrylics.
  • She could print AI-generated textures and collage them into a physical artwork.
  • She might scan hand-drawn sketches and use AI to enhance or stylize them.

Example: In her "Fusion Portraits" series, she:

  1. Used AI to generate abstract facial features.
  2. Manually drew eyes and lips to add a human touch.
  3. Applied watercolor washes to blend the digital and analog.

Strategy 4: Experiment with "AI + Sound" Generation

Sound has a profound impact on creativity, and González often uses ambient noise, music, or even silence to influence AI art. You can try this:

  • Play a specific song while generating AI art.
  • Use white noise or binaural beats to induce different moods.
  • Record your own voice describing the piece and feed it into the AI.

Tool Suggestion:

  • Midjourney (with custom sound prompts)
  • DALL·E 3 (experimenting with "mood-based" audio inputs)

Strategy 5: Create "AI Style Guides" for Consistency

If you’re working on a series or brand, AI can help maintain visual consistency. González does this by:

  1. Generating a "style reference" (e.g., a color palette, typography, or composition rule).
  2. Training a custom AI model (if possible) on her existing work.
  3. Using the same prompts across multiple pieces to ensure cohesion.

Example: If you’re designing a fantasy world, you might:

  • Define a color scheme (e.g., deep greens, golds, and purples).
  • Set composition rules (e.g., "all landscapes must include a central mountain").
  • Use AI to generate consistent characters and environments within those guidelines.

Strategy 6: Use AI for "Reverse Engineering" Old Art

González sometimes scans old paintings or sketches, then uses AI to:

  • Enhance faded colors.
  • Restore damaged sections.
  • Generate variations based on the original style.

How to Do It:

  1. Digitize your artwork (scan or photograph it).
  2. Use AI restoration tools (like DeepImageJ or NightCafe Studio).
  3. Experiment with "what if" scenarios (e.g., "What if this painting was in a cyberpunk style?").

Strategy 7: Collaborate with AI "Artists" in Real Time

Some AI tools allow live interaction, where you can adjust parameters on the fly. González uses this for:

  • Dynamic compositions (e.g., moving elements in real time).
  • Generative art installations (where AI responds to audience input).
  • Interactive digital paintings (where the AI "paints" as you draw).

Tools to Try:

  • Runway ML (for real-time AI video effects)
  • Stable Diffusion WebUI (with live parameter adjustments)
  • AI-Mosaic Studio’s "Live Canvas" (for interactive AI art generation)

Strategy 8: Document Your AI Art Process

González keeps a detailed log of every AI-generated piece, including:

  • The exact prompts used.
  • The AI model version.
  • The emotional and technical decisions behind each step.

This helps her:

  • Recreate past works if needed.
  • Identify patterns in what works and what doesn’t.
  • Share her process with others (which builds trust in AI art).

Action Step:

  • Use a notebook or digital doc to track:
    • Prompt: "A surreal landscape with floating islands and bioluminescent forests."
    • AI Model: Stable Diffusion 1.5
    • Parameters: CFG scale 7.5, seed 12345
    • Refinements: "Added more depth to the sky, adjusted color temperature."
