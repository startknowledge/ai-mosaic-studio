8 Actionable Strategies Inspired by Dolores González’s AI Art Approach

If you want to harness the power of AI like González does, here are eight proven strategies to integrate AI into your creative workflow effectively.

1. Start with a Clear Creative Vision Before Using AI

Problem: Many artists jump into AI tools without a clear direction, leading to generic, uninspired outputs.

González’s Approach: Before generating art with AI, define your artistic intent. Ask yourself:

  • What emotion do I want to evoke?
  • What story or message should the artwork convey?
  • What style (e.g., surrealism, cyberpunk, minimalism) aligns with my brand?

Example: A marketing team wanted to create AI-generated social media graphics for a wellness brand. Instead of just prompting "healthy lifestyle," they refined their input to: "A serene, sunlit forest with glowing mushrooms—vibrant greens, soft pastels, and a sense of tranquility. Inspired by 19th-century botanical illustrations but with a modern digital touch."

This narrowed focus led to highly cohesive, brand-aligned visuals that resonated with their audience.

Actionable Tip:

  • Brainstorm 3-5 key words that define your vision before generating.
  • Sketch a rough concept (even a rough doodle) to guide the AI.
  • Test multiple prompts to see which one aligns best with your vision.

2. Use AI as a Collaborative Tool, Not a Replacement

Problem: Some artists rely too heavily on AI, losing their unique voice in the process.

González’s Approach: González believes in "AI-assisted art"—where the machine enhances human creativity rather than dictates it. Her workflow involves:

  1. Generating multiple AI drafts (10-20 variations).
  2. Selecting the strongest base from the AI output.
  3. Manually refining (color correction, composition tweaks, adding textures).

Example: A digital illustrator used Stable Diffusion to generate character designs for a fantasy novel. Instead of stopping at the AI’s output, she:

  • Kept the AI’s facial structure (which was expressive).
  • Redrew the clothing and background in her own style.
  • Added hand-painted details (like cracks in armor) to make it unique.

The result? A hybrid piece that felt both AI-generated and deeply personal.

Actionable Tip:

  • Treat AI as a "first draft" tool—not the final product.
  • Combine AI with traditional media (e.g., AI-generated sketches + watercolor).
  • Experiment with "AI + human" hybrid techniques (e.g., AI textures + hand-painted layers).

3. Master Prompt Engineering for Better AI Outputs

Problem: Poorly phrased prompts lead to blurry, irrelevant, or off-brand AI art.

González’s Approach: González teaches "structured prompting"—a method where you break down your vision into specific, detailed components. Her golden rule:

"The more precise your prompt, the more aligned the AI’s output will be with your intent."

Example: Instead of: ❌ "A futuristic city"

Use: ✅ "A neon-lit cyberpunk metropolis at dusk, with holographic billboards, towering skyscrapers, and a lone robot walking through the rain. Ultra-detailed, cinematic lighting, inspired by Blade Runner but with a 2024 aesthetic. 8K resolution, hyper-realistic textures."

Advanced Prompting Techniques:

  • Use "style references" (e.g., "in the style of Zdzisław Beksiński but with a digital twist").
  • Specify lighting (e.g., "soft diffused light," "harsh spotlight").
  • Include mood keywords (e.g., "mysterious," "whimsical," "darkly romantic").
  • Avoid vague terms like "beautiful"—instead, say "vibrant, saturated colors with a painterly texture."

Actionable Tip:

  • Save your best prompts in a notebook or digital doc for future use.
  • Test variations (e.g., "minimalist version," "hyper-detailed version").
  • Use AI prompt generators (like Lexica, DreamStudio, or MidJourney’s own prompt library) to refine your language.

4. Leverage AI for Batch Creation (Without Losing Quality)

Problem: Many creators struggle to scale their work while maintaining consistency.

González’s Approach: González uses AI for batch generation—creating multiple variations of an asset quickly—then manually curates the best ones. This is perfect for:

  • Social media content (posts, stories, reels)
  • UI/UX design assets (buttons, icons, mockups)
  • Book covers & product packaging

Example: A self-published author used DALL·E 3 to generate 100+ book cover variations in minutes. Instead of manually designing each one, she:

  1. Generated a base style (e.g., "vintage sci-fi, 1950s pulp novel").
  2. Varied elements (e.g., different fonts, color schemes, background textures).
  3. Selected the top 10 and refined them in Photoshop.

This saved weeks of work while ensuring high-quality, cohesive branding.

Actionable Tip:

  • Use AI for "exploration"—generate many options before committing.
  • Apply filters (e.g., "only include covers with a red spine").
  • Combine AI with templates (e.g., pre-made Canva templates + AI-generated elements).

5. Ethical AI Art: Avoiding Copyright Issues

Problem: AI-generated art can unintentionally infringe on copyright if trained on proprietary datasets.

González’s Approach: González is a strong advocate for ethical AI art, emphasizing: ✔ Using open-source models (e.g., Stable Diffusion, MidJourney’s free tier). ✔ Avoiding direct copies of existing art (e.g., "in the style of Picasso" is riskier than "abstract, geometric shapes with bold colors"). ✔ Checking for AI-generated content policies (e.g., some platforms ban AI art in contests).

Example: A designer tried to generate "a logo in the exact style of Apple’s 1990s logo" using MidJourney. The AI produced a near-identical replica, which could have led to a copyright strike. Instead, González’s team:

  • Described the aesthetic ("minimalist, clean lines, gradient effects, early 2000s tech").
  • Avoided direct references to specific brands.
  • Used a mix of open-source fonts in the final design.

Actionable Tip:

  • Stick to "inspired by" rather than "copying"—describe styles broadly.
  • Use AI for original concepts, not direct replicas.
  • Check AI platform terms—some (like DALL·E) have restrictions on commercial use.

6. AI + Traditional Media: The Hybrid Workflow

Problem: Some artists feel AI art lacks authenticity compared to handcrafted work.

González’s Approach: González combines AI with traditional techniques to create unique, high-value art. Some of her favorite hybrid methods:

  • AI-generated sketches + ink painting
  • AI textures + watercolor washes
  • AI character designs + digital painting

Example: A concept artist used Stable Diffusion to generate a fantasy creature, then:

  1. Kept the AI’s base silhouette.
  2. Redrew the fur texture by hand in Procreate.
  3. Added a glowing aura effect using Photoshop’s brushes.

The result? A piece that felt both AI-assisted and deeply personal.

Actionable Tip:

  • Start with AI for rough drafts, then refine manually.
  • Use AI for tedious tasks (e.g., background details, repetitive elements).
  • Experiment with "AI + physical media" (e.g., AI-generated patterns printed on fabric).

7. Automate Repetitive Tasks with AI Workflows

Problem: Artists waste time on menial tasks (e.g., resizing images, generating thumbnails).

González’s Approach: González automates repetitive tasks using AI tools + automation scripts. Some of her favorite shortcuts:

  • Auto-generating thumbnails for YouTube/TikTok using MidJourney + Batch Processing.
  • Creating social media carousels with AI-generated slides.
  • Generating product mockups (e.g., "a phone with my app interface").

Example: A digital marketer used Zapier + MidJourney to:

  1. Auto-generate 5 social media post ideas per week.
  2. Schedule them via Buffer without manual uploads.
  3. Track engagement to refine future prompts.

This saved 10+ hours per week while maintaining high-quality content.

Actionable Tip:

  • Use AI + automation tools (e.g., AutoGPT, Make.com, Zapier).
  • Batch-process AI outputs (e.g., generate 50 thumbnails at once).
  • Set up templates for recurring content (e.g., weekly blog graphics).

Problem: Many creators fall behind because they don’t track AI advancements.

González’s Approach: González actively follows AI art trends and tests new tools before they become mainstream. Some of her favorite ways to stay updated:

Common Mistakes in AI Art (And How to Avoid Them)

Even experienced creators make mistakes when using AI. Here are the biggest pitfalls—and how González avoids them.

❌ Mistake 1: Over-Reliance on AI Without Human Touch

Why it’s bad: AI art can look generic, repetitive, or uninspired if not refined.

González’s Fix:

  • Always add a human layer (color correction, composition tweaks).
  • Use AI as a tool, not a crutch.

❌ Mistake 2: Ignoring Ethical AI Practices

Why it’s bad: Copyright strikes, AI bias, and unethical training can ruin your reputation.

González’s Fix:

  • Avoid direct copies of existing art.
  • Use open-source models when possible.
  • Check platform policies before commercial use.

❌ Mistake 3: Poor Prompting Leads to Bad Outputs

Why it’s bad: Vague prompts = blurry, irrelevant, or off-brand art.

González’s Fix:

  • Be specific (describe lighting, mood, style).
  • Test multiple variations before committing.

❌ Mistake 4: Not Backing Up AI-Generated Work

Why it’s bad: AI platforms can delete accounts, losing your work.

González’s Fix:

  • Save all AI outputs (download PNGs, JPEGs).
  • Use cloud storage (Google Drive, Dropbox) for backups.

❌ Mistake 5: Forgetting AI Has Limitations

Why it’s bad: AI can’t replace deep creativity—it’s a tool, not a replacement.

González’s Fix:

  • Use AI for exploration, not final products.
  • Combine with traditional techniques for unique results.
