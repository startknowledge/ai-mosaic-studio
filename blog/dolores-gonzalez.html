Strategy 1: Use AI as a "Creative Sparring Partner" (Not Just a Tool)

What It Means: Instead of treating AI as a replacement for brainstorming, use it as a dynamic collaborator that challenges your ideas.

How Dolores González Does It: She often feeds AI vague, open-ended prompts (e.g., "A futuristic city that feels nostalgic") and then iterates based on the AI’s suggestions. This forces her to refine her vision in ways she might not have considered alone.

Real-World Example: A graphic designer working on a brand identity for a sustainable fashion startup might:

  1. Start with a broad prompt: "Minimalist, eco-friendly fashion logo with a touch of vintage charm."
  2. The AI generates 5 variations.
  3. The designer picks the strongest elements from each (e.g., the color palette from one, the typography from another).
  4. The AI then refines a hybrid version, leading to a unique, cohesive design.

Actionable Tip:

  • Start with ambiguous prompts (e.g., "A dark fantasy landscape with a hint of hope").
  • Ask the AI to "contradict" your initial idea (e.g., "Now make it bright and chaotic").
  • Combine the best elements from multiple outputs.

Strategy 2: Train Your AI on Your Personal Artistic Style

What It Means: Most AI art tools (like DALL·E, MidJourney, or Stable Diffusion) rely on generalized datasets. But Dolores González fine-tunes AI models to match her specific artistic voice.

How She Does It: She uses custom training datasets (e.g., her past sketches, favorite artists, and mood boards) to shape the AI’s output. For example:

  • If she loves Cy Twombly’s scribbles, she trains the AI on his works before generating new pieces.
  • If she’s working on a surreal project, she feeds the AI a mix of Dalí and Hieronymus Bosch for inspiration.

Real-World Example: A digital illustrator struggling with consistent character design could:

  1. Collect 20-30 of their best character sketches.
  2. Upload them to an AI training tool (like Stable Diffusion’s "LoRA" feature).
  3. Prompt the AI: "Generate 3 new characters in the style of my uploaded sketches, but with cyberpunk elements."
  4. Use the AI as a "style guide" for future work.

Actionable Tip:

  • Curate a "style library" (e.g., a Google Drive folder with your favorite art references).
  • Use tools like MidJourney’s "Style Reference" or Stable Diffusion’s LoRA to train AI on your work.
  • Experiment with "style blending" (e.g., mix your style with a famous artist’s).

What It Means: AI can analyze emotional cues in text and translate them into visual art—something Dolores González calls "affective generation."

Real-World Example: A film composer working on a tragic love story might:
