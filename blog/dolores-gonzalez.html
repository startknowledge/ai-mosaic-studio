Chapter 2: 8 Actionable AI Strategies for Artists & Designers (Inspired by Dolores González)

If you’re an artist or designer looking to integrate AI into your workflow, Dolores González’s methods provide a clear, ethical, and practical roadmap. Below are eight strategies she advocates, along with step-by-step implementations.

Strategy 1: Start with a Clear Creative Brief (Before Using AI)

Problem: Many artists jump into AI tools without defining their intentions, leading to vague or uninspired results.

González’s Approach: Before generating AI art, ask yourself:

  • What emotion or message do I want to convey?
  • What style or reference points should I use?
  • What are the key elements I want to emphasize?

Example: A client wanted an AI-generated illustration of a futuristic city. Instead of just typing "futuristic city", González refined the prompt to: "A cyberpunk metropolis at dusk, neon signs reflecting on rain-soaked streets, inspired by Blade Runner 2049 and Japanese anime, ultra-detailed, cinematic lighting, 8K resolution."

Actionable Step:

  • Use a prompt template like: "[Subject] in the style of [artist/film], [mood], [key details], [resolution]."

Strategy 2: Layer AI with Traditional Techniques

Problem: Pure AI-generated art can sometimes feel too uniform or "machine-like."

González’s Solution: Combine AI with manual techniques to add human touch:

  • Sketch first, then refine with AI (e.g., use MidJourney to enhance a rough pencil drawing).
  • Use AI for textures, then paint over them (e.g., generate a brick wall texture, then hand-paint details).
  • AI for concept art, traditional media for final polish.

Real-World Example: A client needed concept art for a fantasy game. González:

  1. Sketched a rough character design by hand.
  2. Used DALL·E to generate 10 variations of the character in different poses.
  3. Selected the best AI version, then hand-painted the final textures to make it unique.

Actionable Step:

  • Try "AI-assisted sketching"—use tools like Stable Diffusion’s "Inpainting" to refine rough ideas.

Strategy 3: Experiment with AI Style Transfer

Problem: Artists often struggle to match a specific artistic style (e.g., Van Gogh, Picasso, or cyberpunk).

González’s Hack: Use AI style transfer to:

  • Apply a famous artist’s style to your own work.
  • Blend multiple styles for a unique aesthetic.

Tools to Try:

  • DeepDream (Google’s AI) – For surreal, dreamlike effects.
  • NightCafe Studio – Offers pre-trained style models.
  • Runway ML – Allows custom style transfer with uploaded reference images.

Example: An artist wanted to create a portrait in the style of Hokusai. González:

  1. Uploaded a reference Hokusai woodblock print.
  2. Used NightCafe’s style transfer to apply the brushwork to a modern photo.
  3. Manually adjusted colors to match Hokusai’s palette.

Actionable Step:

  • Find a "style reference" image (e.g., a famous painting) and upload it to an AI tool to test style transfer.

Strategy 4: Use AI for Mood Boards & Inspiration

Problem: Coming up with new ideas can be overwhelming.

González’s Workflow: AI can generate mood boards in seconds, saving hours of manual research.

How to Do It:

  1. Input keywords (e.g., "dark fantasy, gothic architecture, medieval weapons").
  2. Use tools like:
    • Pinterest AI (for visual inspiration).
    • ArtBreeder (to mix styles).
    • Leonardo.AI (for AI-generated mood boards).

Example: A fashion designer needed inspiration for a gothic-inspired collection. González:

  • Generated 20 AI images combining "Victorian mourning attire, steampunk, cyberpunk" in Leonardo.AI.
  • Selected 5 strongest concepts and manually refined them into a cohesive mood board.

Actionable Step:

  • Try "AI mood board generation"—use Leonardo.AI’s "Mood Board" feature to mix styles.

Strategy 5: Automate Repetitive Design Tasks with AI

Problem: Artists spend too much time on repetitive tasks (e.g., resizing, color correction, pattern generation).

González’s Efficiency Hacks:

  • AI for pattern generation (e.g., DeepDream for abstract patterns).
  • Automated color palettes (e.g., Adobe Color + AI suggestions).
  • Batch processing (e.g., MidJourney’s "Variations" feature to generate multiple similar images).

Example: A textile designer needed 100 unique abstract patterns for a fabric line. Instead of drawing each one, González:

  1. Used MidJourney to generate 50 abstract patterns with prompts like "tribal geometry, psychedelic, high contrast."
  2. Selected the best 20, then manually tweaked them in Photoshop.
  3. Exported as a seamless pattern for printing.

Actionable Step:

  • Try "AI pattern generation"—use DALL·E or MidJourney with prompts like "seamless fabric pattern, [style]."

Strategy 6: Collaborate with AI for Brainstorming

Problem: Artist’s block is real, and traditional brainstorming can be slow.

González’s Brainstorming Method:

  1. Input a vague idea (e.g., "a futuristic animal hybrid").
  2. Let AI generate multiple variations (e.g., "cyberpunk fox, steampunk dragon, bioluminescent whale").
  3. Pick the most intriguing concept and refine it manually.

Tools to Use:

  • ChatGPT (for text-based brainstorming).
  • DALL·E (for visual brainstorming).
  • Runway ML (for interactive AI prompts).

Example: A game developer was stuck on character designs. González:

  • Asked ChatGPT: "Give me 10 unique fantasy creature concepts blending medieval and sci-fi elements."
  • Generated 5 AI images based on the best suggestions.
  • Selected one, then drew the final character in Procreate.

Actionable Step:

  • Use AI for "idea sprints"—ask ChatGPT for 10 creative prompts in your niche.

Strategy 7: Protect Your IP with AI (Ethical Considerations)

Problem: AI-generated art can unintentionally infringe on copyright or blur ownership.

González’s Ethical Framework:

  1. Use original references (not copyrighted images) in AI prompts.
  2. Watermark AI-generated work before sharing.
  3. Document your process (e.g., keep a log of prompts used).
  4. Consider AI as a "collaborator"—credit the tool in your work.

Example: A client wanted an AI-generated logo but was concerned about trademark issues. González:

  • Used MidJourney with prompts like "minimalist logo, abstract, no copyrighted elements".
  • Manually refined the design to ensure uniqueness.
  • Added a small AI credit in the corner (e.g., "Generated with MidJourney").

Actionable Step:

  • Always use "original" references—avoid direct copies of famous artworks.

Strategy 8: Monetize AI Art Ethically

Problem: Many artists don’t know how to sell AI-generated work without legal risks.

González’s Monetization Strategies:

  1. Hybrid Art (AI + Human Touch) – Sell AI-assisted work as "collaborative art."
  2. Custom AI Services – Offer AI-generated illustrations for clients.
  3. NFTs (Carefully!) – If selling digital art, disclose AI use to buyers.
  4. AI Tools as a Service – Create custom AI presets for other artists.

Example: A digital artist used AI Mosaic Studio’s custom AI models to generate unique portrait commissions. Instead of selling pure AI art, they:

  • Used AI for the base sketch.
  • Hand-painted textures and details.
  • Marked it as "AI-assisted human art" in their portfolio.

Actionable Step:

  • Start a "hybrid art" portfolio—mix AI and manual work to stand out.
