8 Actionable Strategies to Leverage AI in Creative Projects (Inspired by Dolores González)

Dolores González’s approach to AI art isn’t just about plugging in prompts and hitting "generate." It’s about strategic, ethical, and creative problem-solving. Here are eight proven strategies from her methodology that you can apply to your own projects.

1. Start with a Strong Concept Before Using AI

Problem: Many artists jump straight into AI tools without a clear vision, leading to generic, uninspired outputs.

Dolores’s Solution: "AI is a tool, not a replacement for human thought. The best AI art begins with a conceptual framework."

How to Apply This:

  • Define your theme (e.g., "cyberpunk dystopia," "biophilic minimalism").
  • Research mood boards (use Pinterest, Behance, or ArtStation for inspiration).
  • Develop a style guide (e.g., "high contrast, surrealist elements").

Example: A client wanted an AI-generated album cover for a synthwave band. Instead of just typing "synthwave album cover," Dolores first:

  • Studied retro 80s aesthetics (colors, typography, lighting).
  • Sketched three rough concepts by hand.
  • Used MidJourney with refined prompts like:

    "Cyberpunk synthwave album cover, neon pink gradients, 80s retro-futuristic typography, high contrast, cinematic lighting, inspired by Vangelis and Jean-Michel Jarre, 8K, ultra-detailed."

Result: A unique, marketable design that stood out from generic AI outputs.

2. Use AI for Iteration, Not Just Generation

Problem: AI can produce one-off images, but artists need multiple variations to refine their work.

Dolores’s Solution: "Treat AI like a digital sketchbook—use it to explore ideas quickly, then refine manually."

How to Apply This:

  • Generate 5-10 variations of a concept.
  • Select the strongest base and edit it in Photoshop/Procreate.
  • Use AI for minor tweaks (e.g., adjusting colors, adding details).

Example: A product designer needed 10 different logo variations for a tech startup. Instead of designing each one from scratch, she:

  1. Used DALL·E 3 to generate 10 rough logo concepts with prompts like:

    "Minimalist tech logo, futuristic, geometric shapes, corporate branding, clean lines, 3D render, high resolution."

  2. Selected the top 3 and refined them in Adobe Illustrator.
  3. Used AI (like Adobe Firefly) to tweak colors and textures for final approval.

Result: Faster iteration, lower cost, and a stronger final design.

3. Combine AI with Traditional Techniques

Problem: Pure AI art can feel cold and mechanical without human touch.

Dolores’s Solution: "The most compelling art comes from fusion—AI handles the heavy lifting, but humans add soul."

How to Apply This:

  • Use AI for backgrounds (e.g., surreal landscapes).
  • Hand-paint foreground elements (e.g., characters, textures).
  • Blend digital and analog (e.g., scan hand-drawn sketches and AI-enhance them).

Example: An illustrator wanted to create a "digital watercolor portrait." She:

  1. Used MidJourney to generate a base portrait with:

    "Realistic portrait, watercolor texture, soft lighting, pastel colors, 8K, highly detailed."

  2. Imported it into Procreate and redrew key features (eyes, mouth) by hand.
  3. Added watercolor brushes for a hybrid effect.

Result: A unique, textured piece that felt both AI-generated and handcrafted.

4. Optimize Prompts for Better AI Outputs

Problem: Vague prompts lead to unpredictable, off-brand results.

Dolores’s Solution: "The more specific and structured your prompt, the better the AI’s output."

How to Apply This:

  • Use the "5W Method" (Who, What, Where, When, Why).
  • Include style references (e.g., "inspired by Hokusai, Van Gogh").
  • Specify technical details (e.g., "8K, cinematic lighting, ultra-detailed").

Example: A game developer needed AI-generated fantasy creatures. Instead of:

"Fantasy creature"

They used:

"Epic fantasy creature, inspired by Studio Ghibli and John Howe’s Lord of the Rings illustrations, intricate armor, glowing eyes, detailed fur texture, 8K, hyper-realistic, cinematic lighting, fantasy world, highly detailed, concept art."

Result: High-quality assets ready for Unreal Engine or Blender.

5. Use AI for Mood Boards and Inspiration

Problem: Artists struggle with blocking creativity when starting a project.

Dolores’s Solution: "AI can generate mood boards in seconds, helping you visualize directions before committing to a style."

How to Apply This:

  • Ask AI for "style references" (e.g., "Show me 5 surrealist artworks in a mood board").
  • Use tools like Stable Diffusion’s "img2img" to create variations of a theme.
  • Save inspirations in a digital scrapbook (Notion, Miro, or Pinterest).

Example: A film director was stuck on visuals for a sci-fi film. She:

  1. Asked MidJourney:

    "Sci-fi cityscape, cyberpunk neon, floating islands, futuristic architecture, inspired by Blade Runner 2049 and Tron, ultra-detailed, cinematic, 8K."

  2. Generated 10 variations and selected the strongest elements.
  3. Used them as a reference for her pre-visualization team.

Result: Faster concept development and a cohesive visual identity.

6. Experiment with AI-Generated Textures and Patterns

Problem: Creating unique textures manually is time-consuming.

Dolores’s Solution: "AI can generate infinite textures—use them for backgrounds, fabrics, and materials."

How to Apply This:

  • Use Stable Diffusion or DALL·E to create textures (e.g., "grungy paper texture," "metallic rust texture").
  • Apply them in Photoshop for 3D models, UI designs, or print art.
  • Combine with procedural generation (e.g., Substance Designer + AI).

Example: A fashion designer needed unique fabric patterns for a digital clothing line. She:

  1. Generated 20+ textures with:

    "Abstract fabric pattern, digital art, psychedelic, high resolution, seamless texture, 4K."

  2. Selected the best ones and applied them in Blender for 3D clothing.
  3. Used AI to tweak colors for different collections.

Result: Faster prototyping and exclusive digital fabrics.

7. Collaborate with AI as a "Creative Partner"

Problem: AI can feel isolating—artists want human feedback.

Dolores’s Solution: "Treat AI like a junior collaborator—it generates ideas, but humans refine them."

How to Apply This:

  • Run AI prompts in a group (e.g., "What if we combined cyberpunk and Renaissance art?").
  • Use AI to brainstorm (e.g., "Give me 10 surrealist themes for a horror game").
  • Debate AI suggestions to push creative boundaries.

Example: A team of animators was developing a new animated series. They:

  1. Used AI to generate 50+ character designs with:

    "Anime-style characters, diverse, fantasy creatures, unique personalities, detailed, 8K."

  2. Narrowed it down to 10 and had a workshop to discuss which traits worked best.
  3. Finalized designs by hand-drawing over AI bases.

Result: More diverse, engaging characters with stronger storytelling potential.

8. Monetize AI Art Ethically

Problem: Many artists struggle to profit from AI-generated work due to copyright and licensing issues.

Dolores’s Solution: "AI art can be monetized, but it requires strategic licensing and branding."

How to Apply This:

  • **Use AI for custom commissions (e.g., "I’ll generate 3 variations of your logo").
  • **Sell AI-assisted art (e.g., "Hand-painted over AI sketches").
  • **Create NFT collections with AI-generated art + human touch.
  • **Offer AI training services (e.g., "I’ll teach you to optimize MidJourney prompts").

Example: A digital artist started a Patreon where she:

  1. Used AI to generate weekly "art prompts" for supporters.
  2. Hand-drew over AI bases and sold them as digital downloads.
  3. Offered exclusive AI-generated textures as bonus content.

Result: Steady income from AI-enhanced creativity.
