8 Actionable Strategies from Dolores González’s AI Art Masterclass

Dolores doesn’t just theorize about AI in art—she provides step-by-step frameworks that anyone can implement. Below are eight proven strategies from her workshops and publications, adapted for real-world application.

1. The "Human-AI Collaboration Loop" for Creative Brainstorming

Problem: Many artists struggle to integrate AI into their creative process without feeling like they’re "giving up control."

Dolores’ Solution: She uses a "three-phase collaboration loop" where AI acts as a co-creator, not a replacement.

How to Apply It:

  1. Phase 1: Conceptualization (Human-Driven)

    • Start with a vague idea—no need for perfection.
    • Example: "I want an abstract piece that feels like a futuristic dream."
    • Use AI prompt engineering to refine the concept. Dolores suggests starting with broad, emotional prompts rather than overly specific ones.

  2. Phase 2: AI-Assisted Exploration (AI-Driven)

    • Feed the AI your initial concept and ask it to generate multiple variations.
    • Example Prompt: "Create 5 abstract digital paintings in a cyberpunk aesthetic, using neon blues and purples, with a dreamlike, surreal quality."
    • Let the AI suggest unexpected directions—this is where true innovation happens.

  3. Phase 3: Human Refinement (Human-AI Synthesis)

    • Take the best AI-generated elements and manually refine them in tools like Photoshop, Procreate, or MidJourney.
    • Example: If the AI suggests a floating geometric shape, you might paint over it with watercolor textures to add depth.

Real-World Example: A client wanted a brand identity for a wellness app. Instead of starting with a rigid design brief, Dolores used AI to generate 100+ mood board variations. The client selected a bioluminescent forest theme, and the team then hand-painted the final illustrations using AI-generated color palettes.

2. The "Emotion First" Approach to AI-Generated Art

Problem: AI art often feels cold, generic, or lacking depth because it prioritizes technical execution over emotional impact.

Dolores’ Solution: She teaches artists to anchor AI prompts in human emotions before aesthetics.

How to Apply It:

  1. Identify the Core Emotion you want to evoke (e.g., nostalgia, awe, melancholy).
  2. Use Emotion-Triggering Words in your AI prompts:
    • Instead of: "A futuristic city at night."
    • Try: "A melancholic cyberpunk city, where the neon lights flicker like dying stars, evoking loneliness in a hyper-connected world."
  3. Test Multiple Emotional Angles—AI may surprise you with unexpected interpretations.

Real-World Example: A museum exhibition required AI-generated art pieces that reflected climate anxiety. Dolores’ team used prompts like:

  • "A dying coral reef, glowing with the last light of a sinking sun, symbolizing hope in despair." The resulting pieces were viscerally powerful, sparking conversations about environmentalism.

3. The "Mosaic Method" for Complex AI Art Compositions

Problem: Creating cohesive, multi-layered art with AI can be chaotic—how do you ensure all elements work together?

Dolores’ Solution: She breaks down complex compositions into "mosaic layers", where each AI-generated element serves a specific purpose.

How to Apply It:

  1. Divide Your Art into "Mosaic Blocks" (e.g., background, foreground, textural elements).
  2. Generate Each Block Separately with targeted prompts.
    • Example for a fantasy landscape:
      • Background: "A misty mountain range at dawn, with soft pink and gold hues."
      • Foreground: "A lone traveler’s shadow, elongated and dramatic, with intricate embroidery details."
      • Textures: "Vintage parchment cracks and ink stains for an antique feel."
  3. Combine in Post-Processing to create a unified whole.

Real-World Example: For a video game concept art project, Dolores’ team used AI to generate:

  • A sky with a blood moon (prompt: "Apocalyptic sky, with a crimson moon bleeding into the clouds.")
  • Ruins with cracked stone and overgrown vines (prompt: "Ancient temple ruins, half-buried in nature, with moss and ivy.")
  • A central character with cybernetic enhancements (prompt: "A warrior with a mechanical heart, glowing blue veins.") The final piece was visually stunning and thematically rich, used in the game’s opening cutscene.

4. AI-Powered "Style Transfer" Without Losing Originality

Problem: Many artists fear that AI style transfer will make their work look generic or copied.

Dolores’ Solution: She uses AI as a "style guide" rather than a direct replacement.

How to Apply It:

  1. Find an Artist or Movement whose style you admire (e.g., Zdzisław Beksiński, Yayoi Kusama, or Art Nouveau).
  2. Use AI to Generate Variations of that style with your original subject.
    • Example: "A portrait of a woman in the style of Zdzisław Beksiński, but with modern digital textures."
  3. Manually refine to keep your artistic fingerprint intact.

Real-World Example: A client wanted a custom album cover that blended vintage jazz aesthetics with modern cyberpunk. Dolores used:

  • AI to generate jazz-era typography with a neon glow.
  • AI to create a saxophone player with futuristic cybernetic enhancements. The final design was uniquely their own, not a direct copy of any existing style.

5. The "Reverse Engineering" Technique for AI Art Prompts

Problem: Coming up with effective AI prompts can be frustrating—what if you don’t know where to start?

Dolores’ Solution: She teaches "reverse engineering"—breaking down existing art to understand how to replicate (or improve upon) its style.

How to Apply It:

  1. Analyze a Piece of Art you love (even if it’s not AI-generated).
    • What colors dominate?
    • What composition techniques are used?
    • What emotional tone does it convey?
  2. Translate Those Elements into Prompts.
    • Example: If you love Caravaggio’s chiaroscuro, your prompt might be: "A dramatic portrait in the style of Caravaggio, with extreme high-contrast lighting, deep shadows, and a single spotlight on the subject’s face, evoking mystery and intensity."
  3. Experiment with Variations—AI may surprise you with even more dynamic results.

Real-World Example: A client wanted a mystery thriller movie poster. Dolores studied classic film noir posters and used AI to generate:

  • "A femme fatale in a trench coat, backlit by a single streetlamp, with a smoky, grainy texture." The result was so compelling that it was used as the official poster for an indie film festival.

6. AI-Generated "Mood Boards" for Faster Creative Decision-Making

Problem: Artists often spend weeks refining mood boards manually—AI can accelerate this process.

Dolores’ Solution: She uses AI to generate 100+ mood board variations in minutes, then narrows down the best options.

How to Apply It:

  1. Define Your Project’s Core Themes (e.g., "dark fantasy, steampunk, gothic romance").
  2. Use AI to Generate Mood Board Elements:
    • "100 images of dark fantasy architecture, with a mix of medieval and industrial elements."
    • "50 color palettes inspired by gothic romance novels."
  3. Curate the Best Elements into a digital mood board (using tools like Canva, Adobe XD, or even AI-generated collages).

Real-World Example: For a branding project for a dark fantasy book series, Dolores’ team used AI to generate:

  • Character silhouettes in medieval armor.
  • Textures like rusted metal and ancient parchment.
  • Color schemes with deep purples, blacks, and blood-red accents. The final mood board was used to guide the entire visual identity, saving months of manual work.

7. The "AI + Human Hybrid" Workflow for Large-Scale Projects

Problem: Big projects (like book covers, game assets, or album art) require consistency and scalability—how do you maintain quality?

Dolores’ Solution: She uses a "hybrid workflow" where AI handles repetitive tasks, and humans focus on creative direction.

How to Apply It:

  1. AI Handles:
    • Backgrounds, textures, and repetitive elements (e.g., "Generate 50 variations of a fantasy forest background.")
    • Quick concept sketches (e.g., "Sketch 20 character designs in a cyberpunk theme.")
  2. Humans Handle:
    • Final refinements (e.g., adding hand-painted details to AI-generated characters).
    • Storytelling and emotional depth (e.g., ensuring the narrative arc is clear).

Real-World Example: A video game studio needed 100+ character sprites for a fantasy RPG. Instead of hiring a team of artists, Dolores’ team:

  • Used AI to generate base character designs (e.g., "A human warrior with a wolf companion, in a medieval fantasy style.").
  • Then, manually refined the best 20 designs, adding unique armor patterns and facial expressions. This cut production time by 60% while maintaining high-quality results.

8. Ethical AI Art: How to Avoid "AI Plagiarism" and Maintain Originality

Problem: As AI art becomes more popular, concerns about originality and copyright are rising.

Dolores’ Solution: She advocates for "AI-assisted originality"—using AI as a tool, not a crutch.

How to Apply It:

  1. Always Start with Your Own Ideas—don’t let AI dictate your art.
  2. Use AI as a "Spark"—let it inspire, not replace.
  3. Manually Refine—add your artistic touch (e.g., textures, brushstrokes, or personal symbols).
  4. Document Your Process—keep a creative journal of how AI was used to ensure transparency.

Real-World Example: A controversy arose when an AI-generated portrait was mistaken for a real artist’s work. Dolores’ team avoided this pitfall by:

  • Using AI to generate a base concept.
  • Then, painting over it with traditional media (e.g., oil on canvas).
  • Signing the final piece with a note: "AI-assisted, hand-painted by [Artist Name]."

This approach maintained originality while still leveraging AI’s strengths.

Common Mistakes in AI Art (And How to Avoid Them)

Even the most talented artists make mistakes when integrating AI. Here are five pitfalls and how Dolores avoids them:

1. Over-Reliance on AI Without Human Input

Mistake: Some artists let AI generate the entire piece without manual refinement. Solution: Dolores’ rule: "AI should do 70% of the work, humans 30% of the magic."

  • Fix: Always manually edit AI-generated art—add textures, colors, or personal symbols.

2. Using Generic, Vague Prompts

Mistake: Prompts like "Make me a cool picture" produce mediocre results. Solution: Dolores uses specific, emotional, and detailed prompts.

  • Fix: Always include style references, moods, and unique details.
