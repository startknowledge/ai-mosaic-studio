Chapter 2: 8 Actionable Strategies to Master AI-Generated Art (Like Dolores Gonzalez)

Now that we understand Dolores’s background, let’s explore practical, step-by-step strategies to apply AI art techniques in your own work—whether you're an artist, designer, or entrepreneur.

Strategy 1: Start with a Strong Foundation—Traditional Skills Still Matter

Many artists make the mistake of jumping straight into AI without mastering the basics. Dolores emphasizes that AI is a tool, not a replacement for fundamentals.

Why it works:

  • AI generators (like MidJourney or DALL·E) rely on reference images, composition, and color theory.
  • If you don’t understand lighting, anatomy, or perspective, your AI prompts will produce generic or uninspired results.

How Dolores does it: She begins every project by sketching a rough concept—even if she plans to use AI for the final piece. This ensures the AI has a clear direction to follow.

Actionable Tip:

  • Practice traditional drawing (even for 15 minutes daily) to train your eye for proportions and details.
  • Use AI as a collaborator, not a crutch. For example, if you’re designing a fantasy creature, first sketch its basic shape, then refine it with AI.

Real-World Example: A client wanted a cyberpunk cityscape for a video game. Instead of just typing "cyberpunk city," Dolores:

  1. Sketched a rough layout of buildings and neon lights.
  2. Used MidJourney to generate a base image with the prompt:

    "Hyper-detailed cyberpunk city at night, futuristic skyscrapers with holographic billboards, cinematic lighting, ultra HD, trending on ArtStation"

  3. Manually added details (like glowing signs and intricate architecture) to make it unique.

The result? A highly detailed, marketable asset that the game studio used as a reference for their entire environment.

Strategy 2: Master the Art of Prompt Engineering (Dolores’s Secret Weapon)

The quality of your AI-generated art depends 80% on your prompt. Dolores’s "Prompt Engineering Masterclass" teaches artists how to structure prompts for maximum clarity and creativity.

Key Elements of a Great Prompt:

  1. Specificity – Avoid vague terms like "art" or "beautiful." Instead, say:
    • "A cyberpunk samurai with neon tattoos, ultra-detailed, cinematic lighting, inspired by Ghost in the Shell"
  2. Style References – Mention artists, movies, or games for consistency.
    • "In the style of Moebius, with the color palette of Blade Runner 2049"
  3. Technical Details – Specify resolution, lighting, and composition.
    • "8K resolution, shallow depth of field, dramatic chiaroscuro lighting"
  4. Emotional Tone – AI can convey mood if you guide it.
    • "A melancholic alien landscape, soft pastel colors, dreamy atmosphere"

Dolores’s Prompt Template:

"[Subject] + [Style/Inspiration] + [Technical Details] + [Mood/Atmosphere] + [Trending on/Inspired by] + [Artistic Techniques]"

Example:

"A futuristic robot with organic features, cyberpunk aesthetic, inspired by Alita: Battle Angel, 8K resolution, cinematic lighting, ultra-detailed textures, trending on ArtStation, hyper-realistic, 85mm lens"

Actionable Tip:

  • Save your best prompts in a notebook or digital document.
  • Experiment with variations—sometimes swapping "detailed" for "minimalist" changes the entire vibe.
  • Use AI prompt generators (like Lexica or DreamStudio’s prompt library) to get inspiration.

Common Mistake: Many artists use overly complex prompts with too many conflicting elements (e.g., "a medieval knight in a spaceship, with a dragon and a robot"). Fix: Break it into multiple iterations or focus on one strong element at a time.

Strategy 3: Combine AI with Traditional Techniques (Hybrid Art)

Dolores’s "Hybrid Art Technique" involves using AI for base layers and then enhancing them manually. This ensures your work remains unique and high-quality.

How to Do It:

  1. Generate a base image with AI (e.g., a sketch, color palette, or texture).
  2. Refine it in Photoshop/Procreate by:
    • Adding custom brushstrokes
    • Adjusting colors and lighting
    • Including hand-drawn details (like eyes, hands, or intricate patterns)
  3. Export as a high-res file for commercial use.

Real-World Example: A client wanted a custom album cover for a musician. Dolores:

  1. Used DALL·E 3 to generate a moody, abstract portrait based on the artist’s description.
  2. Imported it into Photoshop and painted over the eyes and mouth with a textured brush.
  3. Added a custom typography element (the band’s logo) in a vintage style.
  4. Finalized with a film grain texture for a retro feel.

The result? A one-of-a-kind piece that the band used for their merchandise and social media.

Tools to Try:

  • Photoshop + MidJourney (for digital artists)
  • Procreate + Stable Diffusion (for iPad users)
  • Krita + DALL·E (for open-source alternatives)

Strategy 4: Use AI for Concept Development (Not Just Final Art)

Many artists only use AI for the final piece, but Dolores teaches that AI is best for brainstorming and refining ideas early.

How She Does It:

  1. Generates multiple variations of a concept (e.g., "a fantasy creature, but make it scary," "make it cute," "make it elegant").
  2. Selects the strongest base and develops it further manually.
  3. Tests different compositions before committing to a final design.

Actionable Tip:

  • Run 3-5 AI variations for every concept.
  • Ask "what if?" questions—e.g., "What if this character had cybernetic limbs?"
  • Use AI to generate thumbnails for YouTube or social media before finalizing.

Real-World Example: A game developer was stuck on character designs for their dark fantasy RPG. Dolores:

  1. Generated 10 different fantasy warrior concepts in MidJourney.
  2. Narrowed it down to 3 based on mood and uniqueness.
  3. Sketched over the best one, adding custom armor details and a unique weapon.
  4. Used AI again to generate background elements (like a ruined castle) to match the character’s aesthetic.

The result? A cohesive world that the developers used for marketing and early gameplay previews.

Strategy 5: Monetize AI Art Ethically (Avoiding Copyright Issues)

One of the biggest concerns with AI art is copyright and ownership. Dolores has developed a system to monetize AI-generated work without legal risks.

Her Ethical Monetization Strategies: ✅ Hybrid Works – Only partially AI-generated (e.g., AI base + manual details). ✅ Custom Commissions – Offer personalized AI art for clients (e.g., "AI-generated portrait in your style"). ✅ Licensing & Usage Rights – Clearly define how clients can use the art (e.g., "for social media only" vs. "for merchandise"). ✅ AI + Traditional Copyright – If using AI for textures or backgrounds, ensure the core design is original. ✅ NFTs with Transparency – If selling NFTs, disclose AI usage to avoid disputes.

Real-World Example: A luxury fashion brand wanted AI-generated textile patterns for their collection. Dolores:

  1. Generated 50+ unique patterns using Stable Diffusion.
  2. Selected the top 10 and manually refined them in Illustrator.
  3. Offered a licensing deal where the brand could use the patterns exclusively for one season.
  4. Kept the original AI files as proof of her contribution.

This approach protected her work while providing high-value assets to the client.

Common Mistake: Selling pure AI-generated art as "original" without manual enhancement. Fix: Always add your artistic touch—even if it’s just color correction or minor edits.

Strategy 6: Automate Repetitive Tasks with AI (Save Time & Money)

AI isn’t just for creative output—it can also streamline workflows. Dolores uses AI to:

  • Generate thumbnails for social media posts.
  • Create mockups of her art in different contexts.

Actionable Tip:

  • Use AI for repetitive tasks (e.g., batch-generating variations of a logo).
