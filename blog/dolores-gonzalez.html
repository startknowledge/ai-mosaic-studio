Common Mistakes When Using AI in Art (And How to Avoid Them)

While AI tools can revolutionize your workflow, they also come with pitfalls. Here’s how to avoid the most common mistakes inspired by Dolores González’s approach.

❌ Mistake 1: Relying Too Much on AI Without Human Input

Problem: Some artists let AI generate entire pieces without manual intervention, leading to generic, uninspired work.

Solution:

  • Always start with a human concept (even if it’s just a rough sketch).
  • Use AI as a tool, not a replacement for your artistic vision.

González’s Approach: She never let AI dictate the entire piece—it was always her idea, refined by AI.

❌ Mistake 2: Ignoring Ethical AI Use

Problem: Some artists use AI without proper attribution, leading to copyright issues or misrepresentation of AI’s role.

Solution:

  • Credit AI tools in your work (e.g., "Generated with MidJourney").
  • Avoid passing AI work off as fully human-made without disclosure.

González’s Approach: She always acknowledged AI’s contribution while emphasizing human creativity.

❌ Mistake 3: Overcomplicating the Process

Problem: Trying to train custom AI models or use too many tools can overwhelm beginners.

Solution:

  • Start with simple tools (e.g., MidJourney, DALL·E).
  • Gradually experiment with more advanced techniques.

González’s Approach: She began with accessible tools before moving to custom AI training.

❌ Mistake 4: Neglecting Technical Limitations

Problem: AI tools struggle with fine details, textures, and complex compositions.

Solution:

  • Use AI for broad strokes, then manually refine details.
  • Combine AI with traditional techniques for better results.

González’s Approach: She used AI for textures and lighting, then hand-painted the final touches.

❌ Mistake 5: Not Experimenting Enough

Problem: Some artists stick to one AI tool or style, missing out on new possibilities.

Solution:

  • Try multiple AI tools (e.g., Stable Diffusion, Leonardo.AI).
  • Experiment with different prompts and techniques.

González’s Approach: She constantly tested new AI models to push creative boundaries.
