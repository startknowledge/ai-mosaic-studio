Chapter 2: 8 Actionable Strategies to Master AI-Generated Art (Like Dolores Gonzalez)

Now that we understand her background, let’s explore practical, step-by-step strategies to create high-quality AI art—just like Dolores does.

Strategy 1: Start with a Strong Prompt Engineering Foundation

Problem: Many artists struggle because their AI tools generate vague, low-detail outputs due to poorly crafted prompts.

Dolores’ Approach: She treats prompt engineering like writing a novel—every word matters. Her 5-step prompt framework ensures clarity and creativity:

  1. Define the Subject – "A cyberpunk woman with neon tattoos"
  2. Set the Style – "Hyper-detailed, cinematic lighting, inspired by Moebius and Studio Ghibli"
  3. Specify Composition – "Close-up, extreme detail in the eyes, dynamic background with floating debris"
  4. Add Emotional Tone – "Mysterious, melancholic, yet hopeful"
  5. Include Technical Constraints – "8K resolution, ultra-sharp, no blurry edges"

Example Prompt (Dolores-Style): "A futuristic marketplace at dusk, bustling with holographic vendors selling AI-generated art, ultra-detailed textures, cinematic lighting, inspired by Blade Runner 2049 and Japanese anime, 8K, hyper-realistic, no blurry edges, emotional depth."

Action Step:

  • Practice writing 10 different prompts for the same subject (e.g., "a spaceship").
  • Use Dolores’ free prompt template (available on her AI Mosaic Studio blog).

Strategy 2: Use MidJourney & DALL·E for Concept Development (Not Final Art)

Problem: Artists often expect perfect, ready-to-sell art from AI in one go—but AI is best for ideas, not execution.

Dolores’ Approach: She uses MidJourney for rapid concept generation and DALL·E for refined details. Her workflow:

  1. Generate 5-10 rough concepts in MidJourney.
  2. Pick the strongest one (based on composition, mood, and technical quality).
  3. Refine in DALL·E with higher resolution, color grading, and fine-tuned details.

Real-World Example: Dolores once needed an AI-generated mural for a client’s café. Instead of uploading a final design, she:

  • Used MidJourney to generate 8 abstract café scenes (different styles, lighting, themes).
  • Selected the most visually engaging one (a "steampunk café with floating books").
  • Refined it in DALL·E, adjusting colors to match the café’s brand palette.

Action Step:

  • Never rely on one AI tool—combine MidJourney (for ideas) + DALL·E (for polish).
  • Save your best concepts in a prompt bank for future projects.

Strategy 3: Master Upscaling & Post-Processing for Professional Results

Problem: AI-generated art is often low-resolution or pixelated when exported.

Dolores’ Approach: She never stops at the AI’s default output. Her post-processing checklist: ✔ Upscale with Topaz Gigapixel (for 4K/8K clarity) ✔ Adjust colors in Photoshop (using HSL sliders for natural-looking tones) ✔ Add subtle noise reduction (to avoid AI’s "hallucination" artifacts) ✔ Apply a texture overlay (for depth, using AI-generated brushes)

Example: For a client’s AI-generated portrait, Dolores:

  1. Generated the base image in Stable Diffusion.
  2. Upscaled it to 8K using Topaz.
  3. Adjusted skin tones in Photoshop (avoiding unnatural cyan/magenta casts).
  4. Added a subtle watercolor texture to soften edges.

Action Step:

  • Invest in Topaz Gigapixel ($99 one-time) for free upscaling.
  • Watch Dolores’ YouTube tutorial on AI post-processing techniques.

Strategy 4: Collaborate with AI (Not Just Command It)

Problem: Many artists treat AI as a black box—they input a prompt and expect perfection.

Dolores’ Approach: She treats AI as a collaborator, not a slave. Her collaborative AI workflow:

  1. Start with a rough AI sketch (low detail).
  2. Refine it manually (adding details, adjusting proportions).
  3. Let AI fill in gaps (e.g., "Make the background more atmospheric").
  4. Iterate until the vision aligns.

Real-World Example: Dolores worked with a client who wanted a "dreamlike landscape." Instead of generating a final image, she:

  • Created a rough AI sketch (blurry, abstract).
  • Drew over it in Procreate, adding trees and a river.
  • Asked AI to enhance the mood ("Make the sky feel more surreal").
  • Finalized with manual touches (adding light leaks).

Action Step:

  • Never trust AI 100%—always edit and refine.
  • Use AI for inspiration, not execution.

Strategy 6: Build a Brand Around Your AI Art Style

Problem: Many AI artists lack a recognizable style, making their work blend in.

Dolores’ Approach: She developed a signature aesthetic—cyber-futuristic with emotional depth. Her branding tips: ✔ Pick a consistent color palette (e.g., deep blues + neon accents). ✔ Use recurring motifs (e.g., floating objects, surreal landscapes). ✔ Develop a "signature" AI prompt (e.g., "Dolores-style: hyper-detailed, emotional, cinematic").

Example: Her "Neon Dreams" collection has:

  • Same color scheme (electric purple + black).
  • Same lighting style (high-contrast, dramatic shadows).
  • Same emotional tone (mysterious, introspective).

Action Step:

  • Create a "style guide" for your AI art.
  • Post consistently (same aesthetic across all platforms).

Strategy 7: Use AI for Batch Content Creation (Without Burnout)

Problem: Artists struggle with consistency—either they overwork or underproduce.

Dolores’ Approach: She uses AI to automate repetitive tasks, freeing up time for high-impact work. Her batch-creation workflow:

  1. Generate 20 AI prompts in one session (e.g., "fantasy creatures").
  2. Batch-process in Photoshop (upscaling, color grading).
  3. Schedule posts (using Buffer or Later).

Example: She once created a "100 AI-generated fantasy creatures" series in under 48 hours by:

  • Generating 100 base images in Stable Diffusion.
  • Upscaling in bulk with Topaz.
  • Adding textures in Photoshop (using AI brushes).

Action Step:

  • Set a "prompt marathon" day (generate 20+ images in one sitting).
  • Automate social media posting (save time).
