8 Actionable Strategies from Dolores González for AI-Powered Art Creation

Dolores González doesn’t just theorize about AI art—she practices and refines techniques that anyone can implement. Below are eight proven strategies from her methodology, broken down into step-by-step actionable steps.

Strategy 1: Start with a Strong Reference Library

Problem: Many AI art creators struggle because they feed the AI vague or inconsistent inputs, leading to generic, uninspired outputs.

Dolores’s Solution: "The best AI art begins with the best reference material. If you don’t give the AI clear, high-quality examples, it can’t learn what you truly want."

How to Implement This:

  1. Curate a Reference Collection

    • Gather at least 20-30 high-resolution images that align with your vision.
    • Include variations (e.g., different lighting, angles, styles) to help the AI understand depth.
    • Example: If creating a digital portrait, collect photos of the subject in different moods, poses, and environments.

  2. Use a Mix of Real and AI-Generated References

    • Combine photographs, paintings, and even sketches to train the AI on diverse artistic influences.
    • Real-world example: Dolores once worked with a client who wanted a fantasy-inspired portrait. She provided the AI with:
      • A real photo of the subject
      • Digital paintings of mythical creatures
      • Textures from medieval manuscripts
    • The result? A unique, otherworldly portrait that blended realism with fantasy.

  3. Organize References by Style and Emotion

    • Label your references (e.g., "Warm tones," "Dark fantasy," "Minimalist") to train the AI to recognize patterns.
    • Tool recommendation: Use Google Drive or Notion to categorize and tag references.

Common Mistake: Skipping this step and relying on single, low-quality images leads to blurry, inconsistent AI outputs.

Strategy 2: Fine-Tune AI with Prompt Engineering

Problem: Many users rely on generic AI prompts (e.g., "Generate a portrait"), which produce cliché, unoriginal results.

Dolores’s Solution: "A prompt is like a recipe—if you don’t specify the ingredients, you’ll get a generic dish. The more detailed, the better."

How to Implement This:

  1. Use the "5W + H" Prompt Structure

    • Who? (Subject identity, e.g., "a 30-year-old woman with curly hair")
    • What? (Style, e.g., "digital painting in the style of Zdzisław Beksiński")
    • Where? (Setting, e.g., "in a neon-lit cyberpunk city")
    • When? (Time period, e.g., "vintage 1920s")
    • Why? (Emotion or theme, e.g., "a sense of melancholic nostalgia")
    • How? (Technique, e.g., "hyper-detailed, oil texture, 8K resolution")

    Example prompt for a fantasy warrior portrait:

    "A fierce female warrior in a medieval fantasy setting, standing on a crumbling stone bridge, holding a glowing sword, inspired by Moebius and Frank Frazetta, ultra-detailed, cinematic lighting, 8K, hyper-realistic textures, dramatic shadows, emotional intensity."

  2. Leverage Negative Prompts to Avoid Unwanted Elements

    • Exclude elements that don’t fit your vision (e.g., "no blurry faces, no cartoonish features, no digital glitches").

  3. Iterate and Refine

    • Start with a rough draft, then narrow down based on feedback.
    • Real-world example: Dolores once helped a gaming studio generate AI characters. The initial prompts produced generic fantasy heroes, so she adjusted them to:

      "A rogue with cybernetic enhancements, inspired by Blade Runner 2049 and Japanese anime, dark moody lighting, intricate metal textures, expressive facial details, 4K."

    The result? A unique, marketable character that stood out in the game’s lore.

Common Mistake: Using too vague prompts (e.g., "Make me a cool picture") leads to wasted time and unsatisfactory results.

Strategy 3: Combine AI with Traditional Art Techniques

Problem: Pure AI-generated art can feel cold and robotic—lacking the emotional depth of human-made art.

Dolores’s Solution: "AI is a tool, not a replacement. The best art comes from human intuition + AI efficiency."

How to Implement This:

  1. Use AI for Sketching, Then Refine Manually

    • Let AI generate initial concepts (e.g., rough sketches, color palettes).
    • Then, hand-paint or digitally refine the final touches.
    • Example: Dolores once used Stable Diffusion to generate 100+ portrait sketches for a client. She then selected the best 10 and hand-detailed them in Photoshop, adding textures and emotions that AI couldn’t capture alone.

  2. Blend AI Styles with Your Signature Look

    • If you’re a traditional painter, train the AI on your past work so it mimics your style.
    • Real-world example: A street artist wanted AI to generate mural concepts in his style. Dolores:
      • Collected 10 of his best murals
      • Trained a custom AI model on them
      • Used the AI to generate variations, which he then sketched over to finalize.

  3. Experiment with AI-Assisted Textures

    • Use AI to generate background textures, patterns, or lighting effects, then compose them manually.
    • Tool recommendation: MidJourney + Photoshop for seamless integration.

Common Mistake: Over-relying on AI without human input, leading to generic, soulless art.
