Common Mistakes When Using AI for Creativity (And How to Avoid Them)

Even the most talented creators make mistakes when integrating AI into their workflow. Here are five pitfalls—and how Dolores González avoids them.

1. Mistake: Treating AI as a "Magic Button" for Perfect Art

Why It’s Bad: Expecting AI to always produce flawless results leads to frustration.

How González Fixes It:

  • AI is a tool, not a replacement for skill.
  • Always refine outputs (even if they’re 90% there).

Solution:

  • Set realistic expectations—AI is great for speed and ideas, but human touch makes it unique.

2. Mistake: Ignoring Copyright and Ethical AI Use

Why It’s Bad: Some AI models train on copyrighted art, leading to legal risks.

How González Fixes It:

  • Use AI models trained on public-domain or licensed datasets.
  • Avoid generating art that mimics protected styles (e.g., Disney characters).

Solution:

  • Check AI tool policies before use.
  • Credit sources if using AI in professional work.

3. Mistake: Over-Optimizing for Trends (Instead of Brand Identity)

Why It’s Bad: Chasing viral AI trends (like "cyberpunk" or "watercolor") can make your brand look inconsistent.

How González Fixes It:

  • AI should serve your brand, not the other way around.
  • Train AI on your brand’s style (as discussed earlier).

Solution:

  • Ask: "Does this fit our brand’s long-term vision?" before using AI.

4. Mistake: Not Testing AI Outputs Before Finalizing

Why It’s Bad: Some AI tools produce errors (e.g., distorted faces, incorrect colors).

How González Fixes It:

  • Always preview AI outputs before finalizing.
  • Use multiple AI models to cross-check results.

Solution:

  • Run 2–3 AI tools (e.g., DALL·E, MidJourney, AI Mosaic Studio) and compare outputs.

5. Mistake: Underestimating the Learning Curve

Why It’s Bad: AI tools require practice to master—skipping the learning phase leads to poor results.

How González Fixes It:

  • Start with simple prompts and gradually complex ones.
  • Experiment with different AI models to find what works best.

Solution:

  • Take AI courses (e.g., Udemy’s AI for Designers).
  • Join AI art communities (like Reddit’s r/AIArt) for feedback.
