8 Actionable Strategies Dolores González Uses to Merge AI with Art

Dolores doesn’t just use AI—she redefines how AI can serve art. Here are the proven strategies she employs, along with practical steps you can apply to your own creative or business projects.

1. Start with a Strong Concept Before AI Intervention

Why it matters: AI is powerful, but it doesn’t think—it generates. The best AI art begins with a clear artistic vision.

Dolores’ Approach:

  • She begins every project by asking three key questions:
    1. What emotional or cultural message do I want to convey?
    2. What traditional artistic techniques will I blend with AI?
    3. How will the viewer interact with this piece?
  • She then sketches manually before feeding prompts to AI, ensuring the final output aligns with her intent.

How to Apply This: ✔ For artists: Before using an AI tool like MidJourney or DALL·E, write a detailed brief (e.g., "A cyberpunk landscape with Mexican folk motifs, inspired by Frida Kahlo’s colors"). ✔ For businesses: If using AI for branding or marketing, define the core message before generating visuals. Example: "We want our AI-generated ad to feel warm, trustworthy, and futuristic—like a blend of Scandinavian design and sci-fi."

Real-World Example: Dolores worked with The Museum of Modern Art (MoMA) to create "Fractal Dreams", an AI-generated exhibit where visitors’ biometric responses influenced the art in real time. The project started with psychologists and curators defining the emotional journey they wanted visitors to experience—not just a random AI output.

2. Use AI as a Collaborative Tool, Not a Replacement

Why it matters: AI excels at pattern recognition and style mimicry, but human intuition, storytelling, and emotional depth remain irreplaceable.

Dolores’ Approach:

  • She treats AI like a "digital collaborator"—something that enhances her work, not replaces it.
  • Example: She might use AI to generate a thousand color palette variations, then hand-select the one that resonates most emotionally.
  • She also manually edits AI outputs to add subtle imperfections that make the art feel more human.

How to Apply This: ✔ For artists: Use AI for brainstorming (e.g., generating multiple style references) but refine the final piece manually. ✔ For designers: Let AI handle repetitive tasks (e.g., resizing logos, generating thumbnails) but ensure the brand voice remains consistent. ✔ For writers: Use AI tools like Jasper or Copy.ai to draft ideas, then rewrite for authenticity.

Real-World Example: In her project "Memory Weave", Dolores used AI to generate abstract patterns inspired by ancient Mexican textiles. However, she manually adjusted the proportions to ensure the final piece felt organic, not robotic. The result was an exhibit where each viewer saw a slightly different version of the same AI-generated base, creating a dynamic, interactive experience.

3. Experiment with AI-Powered Hybrid Techniques

Why it matters: The most innovative art today isn’t purely digital or purely analog—it’s a fusion of both.

Dolores’ Approach:

  • She combines traditional media with AI, such as:
    • AI-generated brushstrokes applied to physical canvases.
    • 3D-printed sculptures where AI designs the internal structure.
    • Voice-activated AI that modifies live paintings based on the artist’s spoken words.
  • She often trains custom AI models on her own artwork to preserve her unique style.

How to Apply This: ✔ For painters: Use AI to generate color palettes, then paint by hand with those colors. ✔ For photographers: Apply AI filters to photos, then manually adjust lighting and composition. ✔ For musicians: Use AI to compose melodies, then arrange them traditionally.

Real-World Example: Dolores collaborated with a traditional Mexican talavera (ceramic) artist to create "Coded Clay". She used AI to analyze historical talavera patterns, then generated new designs that the artist hand-painted onto clay. The result was a modern twist on an ancient craft, blending AI innovation with cultural heritage.

4. Leverage AI for Personalized, Interactive Experiences

Why it matters: The future of art is not static—it’s dynamic, responsive, and tailored to the viewer.

Dolores’ Approach:

  • She designs AI art that changes based on user input, such as:
    • Touch-sensitive installations where gestures alter the artwork.
    • AR (Augmented Reality) pieces that respond to the viewer’s location.
    • AI chatbots that generate art based on conversations (e.g., "Tell me about your childhood, and I’ll paint your memories").
  • She believes interactivity makes art more meaningful because it engages the audience emotionally.

How to Apply This: ✔ For digital artists: Use AI + WebGL to create interactive web art (e.g., Three.js + DALL·E). ✔ For marketers: Develop AI-powered quizzes that generate personalized visual content for users. ✔ For educators: Create AI tutors that adjust lessons based on student responses.

Real-World Example: "Echoes of Memory" (2022) was an AI installation at the Venice Biennale where visitors wore EEG headbands that tracked their brainwaves. The AI then generated abstract art in real time, responding to emotional states like joy, sadness, or curiosity. The result was a unique experience for every visitor, making the art truly personal.

5. Train Custom AI Models to Preserve Your Unique Style

Why it matters: Generic AI tools can mimic styles, but they can’t replicate your personal artistic voice.

Dolores’ Approach:

  • She trains her own AI models using thousands of her paintings, sketches, and digital works.
  • This allows her AI to understand her preferences—whether it’s bold colors, specific brushwork, or thematic motifs.
  • She then uses this custom AI to generate variations of her work, ensuring consistency and authenticity.

How to Apply This: ✔ For artists: Use Stable Diffusion’s custom training or Runway ML to fine-tune AI to your style. ✔ For brands: Train AI on your company’s past visuals to generate cohesive marketing assets. ✔ For musicians: Use AI voice cloning (like Voicify) to preserve your vocal style in new compositions.

Real-World Example: Dolores created "StyleDNA"—a custom AI model trained on her 200+ paintings. When she inputs a new concept (e.g., "a futuristic market scene"), the AI generates multiple versions that align with her artistic DNA. This has dramatically reduced her production time while keeping her work unmistakably hers.

Why it matters: Every artist (and creative professional) hits blocks—AI can be a catalyst for new ideas.

Dolores’ Approach:

How to Apply This: ✔ For writers: Use AI to generate random plot twists when stuck on a story. ✔ For designers: Ask AI to combine unrelated styles (e.g., "Victorian architecture meets space exploration"). ✔ For marketers: Use AI to brainstorm unusual ad angles (e.g., "What if our product was a character in a movie?").

Real-World Example: During a creative slump, Dolores asked her AI: "A symphony of colors inspired by the sounds of a rainforest at midnight." The AI generated a series of abstract, glowing patterns that she later developed into a full digital series. This unexpected prompt reignited her passion and led to a new body of work.

7. Combine AI with Cultural and Historical Research

Why it matters: AI can analyze vast amounts of data, but cultural context makes art more profound.

Dolores’ Approach:

  • She researches historical and cultural references before using AI, ensuring her work respects and honors traditions.
  • Example: When working on a Mexican-themed AI art piece, she studies pre-Columbian pottery, colonial-era paintings, and modern muralismo before feeding AI prompts.

How to Apply This: ✔ For artists: Research art movements, symbols, or techniques before using AI for inspiration. ✔ For historians: Use AI to analyze old manuscripts, then visualize findings in new ways. ✔ For educators: Combine AI-generated timelines with student discussions on cultural impact.

Real-World Example: For her project "Roots & Code", Dolores collaborated with an Aztec scholar to understand the symbolic meanings in ancient codices (pre-Columbian books). She then used AI to generate digital interpretations of these symbols, ensuring the work honored the original intent rather than just replicating it.
