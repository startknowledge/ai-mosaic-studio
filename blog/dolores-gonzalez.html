Before jumping into AI tools, define your vision as precisely as possible.

  • She begins every project with a "creative brief" that includes:

    • Technical constraints (e.g., "must fit a 16:9 aspect ratio")

Actionable Steps for You:

  1. Ask yourself:
    • What is the purpose of this artwork? (Branding? Storytelling? Personal expression?)

  2. Use structured prompts (like González’s team does):

Common Mistakes in AI Art Generation (And How to Avoid Them)

Even the most talented creators make mistakes when working with AI. Here are five pitfalls—and how González avoids them.

Mistake 1: Over-Reliance on AI Without Human Input

Problem: Some artists let AI generate everything, leading to cold, robotic-looking art.

González’s Fix:

  • She treats AI as a collaborator, not a replacement.
  • Always refines outputs manually for emotional depth.

How to Avoid:

  • Use AI for ideas, not final products.
  • Add your own brushstrokes, textures, or color corrections.

Mistake 2: Ignoring the "Chaos" Parameter (Too Much or Too Little)

Problem: In tools like MidJourney, the "chaos" setting controls randomness. Too high = unrecognizable mess; too low = boring, predictable art.

González’s Approach:

  • She starts with chaos at 20-30, then adjusts based on results.
  • If the output is too random, she reduces chaos and refines.

How to Avoid:

  • Experiment with chaos values (try 10, 20, 30, 40).
  • Look for the "sweet spot" where the AI generates recognizable but unique art.

Mistake 3: Poor Prompt Engineering

Problem: Vague prompts lead to generic, uninspired art.

Example of a Bad Prompt: "Make a cool picture."

González’s Better Prompt: "A hyper-detailed mosaic of a futuristic city at dusk, inspired by Byzantine art, with neon signs glowing like fireflies, ultra-realistic textures, cinematic lighting, 8K resolution."

How to Avoid:

  • Be specific about:
    • Style (e.g., "Byzantine mosaic," "cyberpunk")
    • Details (e.g., "neon signs," "fireflies")
    • Technical specs (e.g., "8K," "golden ratio composition")

Mistake 4: Not Checking for Bias in AI Outputs

Problem: Some AI models favor certain demographics or styles, leading to unintentional bias.

Example: An AI trained mostly on Western art may struggle with African or Asian artistic traditions.

  • She diversifies training data to include global art styles.
