8 Actionable Strategies Inspired by Dolores González’s AI Art Approach

Dolores González doesn’t just use AI—she teaches others how to think like an artist in the digital age. Below are eight proven strategies from her methodology that you can apply to your own creative work.

1. Treat AI as a Collaborative Tool, Not a Replacement

González’s Philosophy: "AI is the brush in my hand—it doesn’t paint the masterpiece, but it helps me shape it faster and with more precision."

How to Apply It:

  • Start with a human concept before feeding it into AI tools.
  • Use AI for iteration—refine sketches, color palettes, or compositions.
  • Maintain artistic control by guiding the AI with prompts, not letting it dictate the final output.

Example: A client wanted a custom AI-generated portrait for their brand. Instead of uploading a blank canvas, González provided:

  • A reference image (a traditional sketch).
  • A mood board (color schemes, lighting styles).
  • Specific constraints (e.g., "avoid overly digital textures").

The AI generated 12 variations, which González then refined into a final piece that blended human emotion with AI precision.

2. Combine Traditional Techniques with AI

González’s Approach: "The best AI art isn’t just digital—it’s a fusion of old and new."

How to Apply It:

  • Use AI for initial drafts, then hand-paint or edit key elements.
  • Scan traditional artworks and use AI to enhance textures or colors.
  • Experiment with AI-generated brushes that mimic real-world mediums (e.g., oil paint, watercolor).

Real-World Example: In a 2022 collaboration with a Spanish museum, González used AI to:

  1. Digitize a 17th-century painting (reducing noise and restoring details).
  2. Generate AI-assisted variations of the artwork in different styles (e.g., Impressionist, Cyberpunk).
  3. Have human artists manually refine the most promising AI outputs.

The result? A hybrid exhibition where AI acted as a restoration and creative catalyst, not a replacement for human skill.

3. Leverage AI for Personalized Art Experiences

González’s Innovation: "Art should feel unique to the viewer. AI can help create one-of-a-kind pieces."

How to Apply It:

  • Use AI to generate art based on user data (e.g., personality traits, memories).
  • Develop interactive AI tools where users "train" the AI with their own sketches.
  • Create limited-edition AI art tied to real-world events (e.g., a birthday, anniversary).

Example: AI Mosaic Studio launched "Memory Mosaics"—a service where users:

  1. Upload hand-drawn memories (e.g., childhood sketches).

  3. Generates a custom AI portrait that blends their style with AI-enhanced details.

This approach turned personal nostalgia into digital art, proving that AI can make creativity intimate and meaningful.

4. Optimize AI Prompts for Better Results

González’s Secret: "The quality of your AI art depends 80% on the prompt."

How to Apply It:

  • Be specific—instead of "a landscape," try "a cyberpunk forest at dusk with neon mushrooms and a lone robot."
  • Use negative prompts to exclude unwanted elements (e.g., "no blurry edges").
  • Iterate in small steps—refine prompts based on initial AI outputs.

Example: A client wanted an AI-generated logo for a futuristic wellness brand. González’s initial prompt: "A minimalist logo for a 22nd-century spa, featuring flowing water, bioluminescent plants, and a sleek geometric shape. Style: cyber-minimalism. Avoid: clutter, dark colors."

The AI produced three strong variations, which González then adjusted with secondary prompts to perfect the final design.

Common Mistakes in AI-Assisted Creativity (And How to Avoid Them)

Even the most talented artists and designers make mistakes when integrating AI. Here are five pitfalls—and how Dolores González avoids them.

1. Over-Reliance on AI Without Human Input

Mistake: Letting AI generate art without any human refinement. Solution: Always guide the AI with prompts, then manually enhance the best outputs.

González’s Tip: "AI is a tool, not a chef. You’re the one who seasons the dish."

2. Ignoring Copyright and Ethical Issues

Mistake: Using AI to replicate copyrighted art without permission. Solution: Train AI on original datasets or use public-domain materials.

González’s Approach: "If you can’t trace the source, don’t use it."

3. Poor Prompting Leads to Generic Outputs

Mistake: Using vague prompts like "a beautiful landscape." Solution: Be specific—describe details, mood, and constraints.

Example of a Bad Prompt: "A cat."

Example of a Good Prompt: "A cyberpunk alley cat in a neon-lit Tokyo at 3 AM, with glowing eyes and a cybernetic collar. Style: dark fantasy, ultra-detailed."

4. Not Testing AI Tools Before Full Commitment

Mistake: Jumping into expensive AI tools without trial runs. Solution: Start with free/low-cost AI tools (e.g., DALL·E Mini, Stable Diffusion) before investing.

González’s Advice: "Test AI like you test a new brush—don’t commit to a palette until you know its limits."

5. Forgetting Accessibility in AI Art

Mistake: Creating AI art that’s only appealing to a niche audience. Solution: Design for diversity—consider different styles, cultures, and abilities.

Example: AI Mosaic Studio once generated an AI portrait series where:

  • 50% of the models were people of color.
  • 20% had disabilities (e.g., wheelchair users, visual impairments).
  • The styles varied (traditional, futuristic, abstract).

This inclusive approach made the art relatable to a broader audience.
